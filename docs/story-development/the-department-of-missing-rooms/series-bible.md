# THE DEPARTMENT OF MISSING ROOMS
## Six episodes × 4:00 · one narrator · 24-minute complete season

**Tagline:** You have not been evicted. Your home has been corrected.

**Logline:** In a city that folds inconvenient rooms into storage, an obedient housing surveyor finds his sister's sewing room missing from a building he approved. Before its temporary storage is purged at dawn, he must move three occupied rooms into the luxury display suite occupying their emergency receiving bays—and preserve proof that the people inside were never an accounting error.

**Genre balance:** architectural horror supplies the threat; dystopian satire explains who benefits; a sibling rescue provides the emotional through-line. The trippy imagery comes from spatial compression, not random hallucinations.

**Theme:** a measurement becomes violence when the people being measured cannot challenge what it excludes.

## 1. The city and the impossible bargain

After a housing emergency, the city invented **folding**: rooms could be compressed temporarily into building-scale storage, allowing repairs and emergency evacuation without demolishing entire blocks. It is one impossible technology, not a world where any paperwork changes reality.

The Housing Department now uses it to meet contradictory targets: wider fire corridors, more rentable floor area, fewer officially displaced households. Folded residents disappear from the occupancy denominator. Oversized show apartments demonstrate the success of the policy to prospective investors.

The public ideology is **efficiency as fairness**. The moral failure is not measuring space; it is treating the uncounted as nonexistent and leaving a supposedly temporary measure unreviewed.

**Scope:** one building, three folded rooms, nine occupants, one surveyor, one dawn purge. This story rescues those people and documents the practice. It does not reform the whole city in a morning.

## 2. The narrator and silent ensemble

### Orrin Voss, 52 — district surveyor, sole voice

Orrin records inspection reports because the Department distrusts handwritten corrections. His dry precision has made an awful job bearable. His characteristic defense is to replace a human noun with a technical one, then make a joke about the technical noun.

- **Want:** close a discrepancy without reopening his approved plans.
- **Wound:** he once let his younger sister take responsibility for a shared household debt so his certification remained clean. He calls compliance sacrifice, though she paid much of the cost.
- **False belief:** if the number is correct, responsibility belongs to whoever interprets it.
- **Need:** make his own signed contribution visible and accept that knowledge obliges an action.
- **Voice progression:** polished report → irritated correction → personal admission → spoken work log under pressure → factual witness account with no comforting conclusion.
- **Narrative position:** an overnight report recorded as events occur. He may interpret an expression, but does not know another person's unspoken thoughts. The last episode includes a clearly marked three-week follow-up, not knowledge smuggled in from the future.

### Lenna Voss, 47 — Orrin's sister, seamstress

Lenna uses space socially: chairs drawn together, curtains repaired, clothes altered for neighbors. She gave Orrin a stitched measuring tape because his official steel tape could not measure a body comfortably. She knows he prefers a solvable fault to an apology.

She is trapped in one of the folded rooms but remains an agent. She discovers that a maintenance seam transmits touch, sends the building survey's three-tug service signal, and organizes the other occupants to keep the internal release latches clear. She never taps a complete plot explanation in a conveniently decipherable code.

### Mrs. Rill, 71 — silent tenant representative

Keeps old floor plans. Distrusts Orrin before he earns a reason to trust him. She chooses to help evacuate the display suite, not to absolve his signature. Later carries the evidence to other residents.

### The Compliance Attendant — silent institutional antagonist

A human worker in a spotless coat, neither supernatural nor omniscient. Enforces the purge schedule, shows the costs of cancellation, locks authorized controls, and finally refuses to join Orrin in hiding what happened. Does not suddenly become the story's hero.

### The other eight occupants

Visible as distinct bodies and activities through compressed seams: a night porter, a caregiver with an older relative, two laundry workers, and a small family of three. Lenna is the ninth. They are not ghost doubles or nine versions of the same person. Their survival is counted visibly at the end.

## 3. Writer-facing rules

1. **Folding acts through a physical frame and motor.** A form authorizes the machine; a form does not itself erase a person or space.
2. **Rooms remain real and occupied while stored.** Doors and windows compress to seams, so people cannot simply step out. No full-sized person fits through a folded threshold.
3. **Storage is temporary.** Air and utility service are sustained only while the storage frame is connected. At dawn, a scheduled purge disconnects and crushes the stored modules to reclaim the frame. This is the immediate lethal threat. Do not invent a separate arbitrary oxygen deadline.
4. **Volume must be accounted for.** Unfolding three rooms requires three matching empty receiving bays. No spell, speech, or emotional sacrifice can make an extra room appear.
5. **The display suite occupies the original three receiving bays.** Its removable finishes and staging furniture must be cleared. The approved emergency-receiver designation survives beneath a marketing overlay in the original plans.
6. **A mechanical transfer lock routes rooms between storage and receiving bays.** Orrin's survey key can inspect it. A sealed control prevents unauthorized transfer; breaking that seal is visible and leaves his responsibility recorded.
7. **Manual release requires alignment, an external operator, and an internal clear latch.** It cannot be completed remotely by one heroic button press. This is why Lenna's actions and the other residents matter.
8. **Memory is unaffected.** People remember the missing residents. Official records omit occupancy; reality has not erased relationships. No one inexplicably forgets Lenna.
9. **Reports are evidence, not magic.** Orrin's voice preserves names, events, and instructions. It cannot hold a wall open. The physical bypass must be disconnected to stop the purge; public records prevent the incident being quietly recategorized afterward.
10. **Only Orrin is intelligibly heard.** Other people may breathe or make nonverbal exertion sounds, but there are no whispered explanatory lines, secondary intercom voices, or voiced recordings by another actor.

## 4. Images and sound

**Hero object:** Lenna's cloth measuring tape, frayed at the first inch and repaired with yellow thread. Orrin must account for the missing inch rather than treating the repair as exact calibration. The tape locates the seam; it does not stop the motor or carry human weight.

**Other motifs:** red correction lines; wallpaper flowers cut in half by absent doorways; three service knocks; a showroom fruit bowl made of hollow plaster; white dust that falls sideways near compressed frames.

**Sound:** stamp-clicks become motor engagement; three tugs have a dry fabric rasp; storage air pumps continue beneath apparently solid walls. The final silence of the motor is relief, but shutting it down does not silence the city.

**Palette:** municipal cream, correction red, storage bruised violet, Lenna's yellow thread. Lens distortion is tied to proximity to folding hardware. No unrelated dream sequence.

**Performance:** one mature masculine voice, conversational and unhurried, with humor that is less confident as the danger becomes personal. Avoid a permanent spooky whisper; clarity makes the horror worse.

## 5. Complete episodes: picture beats and spoken narration

Read only the **VO** paragraphs as narration. Each paragraph belongs within its listed 45-second beat; the remaining time is picture and sound. Episode titles and production notes are not spoken.

### Episode 1 — “A Minor Discrepancy”

**Local objective:** inspect an unexplained reduction in room count. **Change:** Orrin recognizes a person behind the discrepancy. **Clock:** 22:10, purge at 06:00.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** A corridor opens like a paper model. Orrin checks an occupancy board: 81 corrected to 78. A cheerful poster depicts a family enjoying an impossibly spacious home. In the real corridor two neighbors pass sideways.

**VO:** Our building became three rooms more efficient on a Thursday. Nobody moved out. This was considered an improvement. I was sent to verify the improvement, because the city allows a discrepancy of two rooms before requiring a person. I am that person. My name is Orrin Voss. I used to find the distinction amusing.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Orrin follows the approved plan. A wall interrupts the remembered route. Half a wallpaper flower faces the other half across a hairline crease. He checks behind himself, then checks the plan again.

**VO:** My sister's sewing room was between the laundry and the stairs. I knew because I had carried her machine up both flights and mentioned it every birthday since. The new plan placed the laundry beside the stairs. I stood in the useful space where my sister was no longer supposed to be.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** He calls toward the seam; no answer is intelligible. On the reverse of a maintenance hatch: temporary storage until 06:00. An air pump pulses. He feels moving air at a screw hole.

**VO:** Folding was invented for emergencies. Fire damage. Flooding. Repairs. A room waits in storage while its place is made safe. According to the notice, this place had been made profitable. The storage connection would be purged at six. The notice did not say what the room was meant to do with its people.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** Orrin takes out his steel survey tape. It catches at the crease. From his pocket he retrieves Lenna's soft tape and corrects for its damaged first inch. A few centimeters of its edge enter the seam.

**VO:** Lenna gave me a cloth tape because, she said, buildings were not the only things that needed room. I told her cloth stretched. She repaired the first inch with yellow thread and told me to remember it was missing. I remembered the missing inch more faithfully than I remembered to visit.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** The tape slides inward. Three distinct tugs. Orrin drops his clipboard. For the first time he kneels rather than measures from standing. The yellow thread pulls taut between wall and hand.

**VO:** Something took the other end. Three tugs. The service signal we were taught before the Department replaced training with a video. Not a haunting. Not settling plaster. A person, behind a wall I had certified as empty. I stopped the report. Then I started it again. This time I said her name.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. Hold the seam and the intact tape; no extra whispered voice.

### Episode 2 — “Unoccupied, Technically”

**Local objective:** find where folded rooms are held and how to retrieve them. **Change:** a private rescue becomes nine people and a visible act of noncompliance. **Clock:** 23:00–00:20.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** The silent Attendant shows Orrin a green occupancy total, an expensive purge-cancellation penalty, and an emergency-review slot at 08:00, two hours after the purge. Orrin puts Lenna's tape across the green display. The Attendant removes it carefully, like dirt.

**VO:** The attendant showed me a green number. Green means safe, unless it means complete, paid, compliant, or not our department. The emergency review was at eight. Purge remained at six. I showed the attendant the tape moving inside the wall. They offered to record it as an equipment fault. This is how a city becomes haunted: one accurate description is refused at a time.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Orrin uses his survey key to open an inspection hatch, not the transfer control. Beyond: three folded frames. Nine ordinary personal objects hang in compressed perspective. Air lines run to all three.

**VO:** There were three frames in the storage shaft. Each held a room made thinner than its door. I could see a coat, a lunch tin, a child's shoe. Then I stopped counting possessions and started counting movement. Nine people. My sister was one of them. That made her neither less important nor the only important one.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** Orrin examines physical arrows linking storage to receiving bays. The three receivers have been decorated over on the current plan. His older signed diagram remains underneath. He lines up its corners with the live hardware.

**VO:** A folded room needs somewhere to unfold. It does not care how urgently you ask. The receiving bays were still on the original plan, under a decorative rectangle labeled Demonstration Residence. I recognized the signature approving the rectangle. Recognition is a very small word for discovering you have already been here, holding the wrong pen.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** He unscrews the showroom plan cover and reveals the original emergency-receiver label. He lays three frame templates over three matching suite modules. None is yet clear; each is full of staging furniture.

**VO:** The show apartment had three rooms, all empty of residents and full of things shaped like a life. A bowl of fruit that could not rot. Books that could not open. We had not run out of space. We had used it to demonstrate how much space the city had learned to save.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** The Attendant locks the transfer panel and places a tamper seal. Orrin photographs the seal and his own ID, then breaks it in view of the recorder. The purge clock continues. He does not yet activate transfer.

**VO:** My key could inspect the mechanism. It could not authorize a transfer. The attendant sealed the control and waited for me to become reasonable. I recorded the seal number, my name, and what I intended to do. There are moments when making a mistake properly becomes less useful than making a record of refusing one.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. Broken seal lies beside the live timer.

### Episode 3 — “The Display Family”

**Local objective:** clear the receiving bays. **Change:** Orrin needs help from people who distrust him; trapped residents begin their own part of the rescue. **Clock:** 01:10–02:30.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** Orrin drags a showroom table that is bolted down. His jacket tears. Mrs. Rill arrives, holding the original plan and a tool roll. She does not accept his offered hand; she points to the bolts.

**VO:** I began with the dining table. It was secured against theft, although there was nobody allowed to eat at it. Mrs. Rill brought the building's old plans and a socket wrench. She had spent six months asking me to look at those plans. I had advised her to submit a clearer copy.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Residents unbolt furniture. Orrin carries items instead of supervising. Plaster fruit breaks open; dust falls sideways as a frame vibrates. Three module outlines appear beneath the carpet.

**VO:** We removed the family that had never lived there. Their six chairs. Their reassuring lamps. Their pears, which were hollow. The real residents worked without waiting to forgive me. I had confused those two things before: being permitted to help and being declared a good person. Only one was necessary tonight.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** Through a narrow inspection view, Lenna knots the cloth tape to a latch, touches a second occupant's arm, and demonstrates a three-count gesture. A neighboring room responds. No one becomes visible outside their own room yet.

**VO:** Inside the shaft, Lenna had found the release bar. She passed the signal from room to room. Three taps to prepare. A raised hand to wait. The old maintenance diagrams called this internal clearance. My sister had always been good at explaining a difficult thing without making the other person feel small.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** A test pulse moves one frame a few millimeters. One receiver outline is offset by a fixed partition. Orrin stops the pulse before it strains a frame. The motor falls quiet. The Attendant points to the schedule.

**VO:** We tested the first alignment. The frame moved, caught, and began to strain. I stopped it. It would have been possible to call the noise progress. I know, because I have attended meetings. One partition crossed the receiver line. It had to come down before anything living was asked to pass through it.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** The residents expose the partition's hidden fixings. Behind it, a live bypass cable joins purge control to the frame. Orrin traces it with the plan. Its warning lamp is red. Dawn has not been canceled by stopping the transfer motor.

**VO:** Behind the partition was the purge bypass. Stopping a transfer would not stop the morning disposal. That had been designed separately, to prevent delays. The city had anticipated resistance from equipment. It had not, apparently, anticipated anyone wanting to keep the rooms. We had a place for them now. We still had to keep it.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. A warning lamp keeps time against silent faces.

### Episode 4 — “Nothing Personal”

**Local objective:** stop automatic purge without interrupting the storage life-support lines. **Change:** Orrin gives up the possibility of quietly rescuing only Lenna. **Clock:** 03:00–04:20.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** The Attendant offers an individual extraction authorization for one frame containing Lenna. Two others remain marked disposal. Orrin looks from the form to nine drawn tally marks, then lays the form face down.

**VO:** The attendant found an exception. One room could be recovered as a family accommodation error. My sister's room. The other two would remain a capacity adjustment. I understood the offer perfectly. That was the worst part. There was a version of me who would have called it the best outcome available.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Orrin opens the receiver partition. Mrs. Rill holds the original diagram beside the live cables. He labels purge, air, transfer. He follows each physically instead of pulling the largest connector. Lenna's tape remains visible at the seam.

**VO:** The bypass shared a housing with the air connections. Pulling everything would stop the purge and the breathing. We traced each line. Twice. Mrs. Rill made me point to where it ended, not where the drawing said it should end. This was a slower way to work. It was also the first useful thing I had learned in years.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** He isolates the purge bypass. A clamp unlocks; the air pumps continue. A mechanical indicator retracts. The countdown disappears from the frame itself, but a remote reset warning starts on the Attendant's tablet.

**VO:** The purge connection came free with a noise smaller than I expected. The pumps kept turning. Nobody applauded. We listened to the ordinary movement of air. I had thought courage would feel different from checking a valve. Mostly it felt like checking the valve again, because people were still on the other side.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** The Attendant presents a remote reset request. Orrin shows that the bypass is physically disconnected, photographs the safe isolation, and forwards the recorded inspection to the building's resident terminal. Mrs. Rill verifies receipt and saves a copy.

**VO:** The reset order arrived before my hands stopped shaking. It could restart the schedule, but not reconnect a cable. I sent the report outside my own account. Mrs. Rill checked that it had arrived. Evidence stored only where its author can delete it is sometimes less an account than a private arrangement with the truth.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** Dawn begins to pale the window. Orrin sets manual alignment. Lenna and other occupants clear the latches. A stress gauge rises: storage connections are temporary even without the purge. Everyone is positioned; no rescue has occurred yet.

**VO:** We had stopped the deliberate danger. The temporary frames were still temporary. The seals were warming; the rooms needed to come out before the connections failed. I told the report that nine people remained inside. I said it again while we took our places. This time the number was not an argument for doing less.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. Nine tally marks; first edge of natural morning.

### Episode 5 — “Please Mind the Margin”

**Local objective:** complete the three physical transfers. **Change:** the rescue depends on cooperation rather than Orrin's authority. **Clock:** 04:45–05:55.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** The first transfer starts. A room expands into its original receiver; wallpaper flowers align. Three people step clear after the motor stops. Mrs. Rill counts them, checks the safe line, and holds the next group back.

**VO:** The first room returned one corner at a time. Wallpaper found the rest of its flowers. A door remembered how wide a person was. Three people came out. Mrs. Rill counted them, then made room for them to sit. It was the smallest room in the show apartment. It was no longer wasted space.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** The second frame catches on loose staging cable left behind a baseboard. Orrin stops, points to the mechanical load indicator, and waits while a resident retrieves the cable from the empty receiver side. The frame continues safely.

**VO:** The second caught on a cable we had missed. I wanted to finish. Wanting was not a measurement. We stopped, cleared the bay, and started again. Three more people crossed the line. No one became braver by being hurried. The child carried the shoe I had seen in the shaft, and its ordinary size nearly undid me.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** The third room holds Lenna, the caregiver, and the older resident. Its internal latch remains partly obstructed by a fallen sewing table. Lenna passes the tape to the caregiver and uses both hands to shift the table. Orrin waits at the safe control position.

**VO:** Lenna's release bar would not clear. Her table had fallen across it. I could see her shoulder against the wood. I could not reach her. For once there was no form I could put between myself and that fact. She passed the tape to another pair of hands and made space where she stood.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** The third transfer completes. Two residents exit, then Lenna. Orrin reaches to embrace her; she first turns back for the caregiver's bag. He helps carry it. Mrs. Rill marks the ninth tally only when Lenna is fully clear.

**VO:** Two came out. Then my sister. I reached for her and she went back for someone's bag, because surviving does not immediately stop a person needing their things. I took the other handle. We carried it between us. Nine people. I will not say returned to normal. Normal had done this to them.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** At 05:55, Orrin removes and tags the folding control key, leaves the air circuit live, and bolts the isolated transfer cover open for inspection. The Attendant photographs the violated seal but does not reconnect the bypass. Nine residents are present outside the former storage footprint.

**VO:** At five fifty-five we tagged the mechanism out of service. The attendant recorded the broken seal. I gave them my name without being asked. The morning purge had no rooms left to destroy. There would be consequences. This was the first accurate use of that word in a building that had been full of them all night.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. The former showroom, now crowded with actual people.

### Episode 6 — “Occupied”

**Local objective:** keep the rescue from being reclassified as proof the original policy worked. **Change:** Orrin becomes a witness rather than the owner of the story. **Time:** morning, then a labeled three-week follow-up.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** Officials arrive with a camera and a preprinted success banner. Mrs. Rill holds the timestamped report in front of the banner. Orrin turns his recorder toward the damaged storage frames, not toward a celebration pose.

**VO:** By breakfast, the Department had prepared a successful recovery announcement. I was offered a sentence about swift corrective action. The sentence contained no people who had needed correcting, which was an improvement on the previous plan. Mrs. Rill brought the report. I left the recorder running while the banner came down.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Nine names and occupancy corrections are entered individually. Lenna reads her own record, points to an error in the spelling, and waits until it is fixed. Others collect copies. No one is required to pose gratefully.

**VO:** We recorded nine names, checked by the people who owned them. Lenna corrected a letter in hers. I had been about to tell her it was close enough. The reflex survived the night. I mention that because this is not a report about becoming a different man. It is about finally noticing what the old one does.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** THREE WEEKS LATER. Orrin's credential is suspended pending review. The rooms remain unfolded under a local safety prohibition. Other buildings show unresolved red marks. Mrs. Rill distributes report copies without him at the center.

**VO:** Three weeks later, my certification was under review. The three rooms remained open under a safety order. Other buildings still folded. I cannot honestly finish with the city waking up. What happened here had become harder to deny. That is smaller than justice. It is larger than the private exception I nearly accepted.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** Lenna repairs clothes in a modest communal room. Orrin arrives without a form. He holds a loose curtain rail while she screws it back in. She accepts the help, then points to a chair. Their relationship is still awkward.

**VO:** Lenna did not ask me to narrate what I had learned. She asked me to hold the curtain rail straight. Then she gave me a chair. I sat down without inspecting it. We had tea beside a wall that stayed where it was. I had no useful information to contribute. She seemed pleased by this.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** Orrin records the closing inventory. He measures the doorway with the repaired cloth tape, compensates for the missing inch, and writes the actual figure in the margin. A neighbor's hand adds a correction on another page. He makes room for it.

**VO:** Final inventory. Three rooms recovered. Nine occupants accounted for. One official signature referred for investigation: mine. The cloth tape is still short by an inch. We mark the difference rather than pretending it is perfect. There is room in the margin. There always was. I am learning who the margin was meant to hold.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. A corrected floor plan; ordinary room sound, no final supernatural sting.

## 6. Setup / payoff and continuity audit

| Plant | Payoff |
|---|---|
| 81 corrected to 78 in episode 1 | Three stored rooms discovered in 2; three receiving bays cleared; 81 restored in 6. Room count is not confused with nine occupants. |
| Cloth tape's missing first inch | Orrin compensates correctly in 1 and 6. It remains imperfect and never becomes load-bearing rescue gear. |
| Three-tug maintenance signal | Identifies a living trained sender in 1; organizes internal clearance in 3–5. It never transmits full sentences. |
| Original receiver designation | Establishes destination before rescue. Showroom volume is not invented in the climax. |
| Separate purge bypass | Found in 3, isolated carefully in 4, remains disconnected in 5. No false claim that switching off the transfer motor stops dawn purge. |
| Orrin's own approval | Discovered in 2, distributed in 4, includes his responsibility in 6. He cannot erase his role by rescuing Lenna. |
| Hollow pears | Satirical proof of a lifeless display home; actual residents occupy the reclaimed volume. |

**Three transfers:** first room = night porter plus two laundry workers; second = family of three; third = Lenna plus caregiver and older relative. Total nine. No surprise extra occupant or corpse appears for a last-second emotional cheat.

**Why not call emergency services?** Orrin sends an inspection alert with the report, but the authorized response is a scheduled morning review; the Attendant's local exception is faster but abandons six people. The plot must not pretend phones do not exist. The actual live isolation and receiver work cannot wait for the review. The 08:00 review time is visible in episode 2, before the threatened 06:00 purge; the narrated beat states the mismatch explicitly.

**Why not smash through the wall?** A seam is a compressed threshold, not ordinary plaster concealing a full room. The first inspection view establishes this physically. Drilling the surface will not restore dimensions.

**Why isn't the Attendant physically stopping everyone?** Their authority is procedural and infrastructural, not a private armed force. They lock controls, record violations, and request reset; residents can refuse at documented risk. They are not omnipotent.

**Ending:** all nine survive; occupancy restored locally; dangerous hardware isolated; responsibility and broader practice remain under review. Lenna does not automatically forgive Orrin. The horror's practical cause is resolved without explaining away the horror as a dream.

## 7. Production limits

Four modular environments: corridor, inspection shaft, display suite/receiving rooms, later communal sewing room. One human rig can provide the silent background residents with clearly different silhouettes. The Attendant needs blocking and readable actions, not dialogue animation.

Reserve expensive distortion shots for one folded-frame reveal and one unfolding transfer; use close details, changing scale, air movement, and repeated architectural angles elsewhere. Keep people's limbs anatomically coherent during rescue; the danger is the room, not arbitrary body transformation.

Single narrator; no voiced quoted dialogue. Effects and breaths must not accidentally imply another speaking performer. A full sound-only edition would need additional descriptive narration; this script assumes pictures.
