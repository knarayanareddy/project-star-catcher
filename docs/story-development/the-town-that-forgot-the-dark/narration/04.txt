The first shutter did not close. This was fortunate to discover before we needed it closed. There is a kind of hope that arrives holding a screwdriver. I recommend it over the sort that requires everyone to stop mentioning the hole. We patched the joint and tested it again. The dark stayed where we put it.

We transferred the essential circuits with the clinic staff watching their equipment. The decorative tower went dark. Nothing dreadful happened to the tower. It remained decorative. The pumps held. The cells held. We wrote down what we had tested instead of announcing that we were ready for anything. I had become suspicious of that particular sentence.

My husband died in a winter outage. For years, any darkness felt like agreement with what had taken him. But that night had no warning, no backup, no one at the lower stairs. We were making those things now. Preparing for the dark was not the same as welcoming harm. I wished I had learned that before I had a voice people trusted.

Harl signed the isolation record. It did not unsign the extensions. He gave us the collector key and took a place at the feeding valves. I did not forgive him. He did not ask me to. This was a better arrangement than either of us making a speech about difficult decisions while someone else turned the handles.

We scheduled the hour before sunrise, with fifteen minutes of reserve beyond it. Thirty-one valves. One collector. Shutters checked. Routes marked. I could not promise the sleepers would wake safely. I could promise not to hide them inside a better-sounding number. It was a smaller thing to say. It was finally something I could keep.
