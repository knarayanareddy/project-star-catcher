# Development slate — cross-project context

The original Star Catcher review is now in [its own folder](../star-catcher/arc-review.md). Open an individual series folder for its production reference; this document is optional comparison and historical development context.

## 2. The slate

| Series | Society's sincere promise | Dystopian mechanism | Tone | Central relationship | Viewer question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Good Mourning** | Nobody should suffer alone. | Happiness insurance converts unprocessed grief into profitable dependency. | Warm bureaucratic black comedy turning emotionally raw. | A claims adjuster and the sister she has avoided grieving with. | When does care become control? |
| **The Republic of Tomorrow** | Prevent harm before it happens. | A probabilistic forecast becomes a compulsory script that makes itself true. | Clockwork civic thriller with dry satire. | A forecaster and the brother whose earlier confinement she authorized. | Can a decision be moral if nobody is allowed to make it? |
| **Nobody Owns Tuesday** | Nobody should inherit domination. | People rotate homes and offices while a permanent maintenance caste remembers for them. | Trippy workplace farce becoming a collective heist. | An ambitious rotating citizen and the supposedly unskilled cleaner she cannot remember loving. | Can equality survive continuity, competence, and attachment? |

These are not three versions of “an evil computer lied.” One system sells relief, one governs through confidence in prediction, and one distributes prestige while hiding continuity work. Human institutions, incentives, and choices sustain each.

## 3. Shared format discipline

- Each series tells one complete **60-minute story**, not a pilot stretched into six trailers.
- Episode timecodes are **screen-time allocations**, not a demand to fill dialogue at constant speed. Every episode has five movements totaling 10:00; reserve approximately 15 seconds within the final movement for end card/credits.
- Establish protagonist, social transaction, and contradiction in the first two minutes. Show ideology through a bill, queue, schedule, appointment, or work shift before anyone debates it.
- Limit the principal speaking ensemble to five or six. Give each episode one dominant location and one dominant new fact. No second mythology introduced in the finale.
- Every episode resolves a local objective and creates the next problem. At least one relationship changes irreversibly.
- Humor targets institutional absurdity and self-protective behavior, not grieving people, disability, poverty, or the mere existence of expertise.
- The world rules below are writer-facing canon. Characters may misunderstand them; scripts must not quietly change them to escape a corner.
- No secret “it was all a dream,” omnipotent algorithm, surprise twin, or late-arriving technology will explain the ending.

**Suggested first production:** *Good Mourning*. It needs the fewest complex environments and gives performers the clearest emotional entry point. *Tomorrow* offers the strongest puzzle-thriller engine. *Tuesday* offers the most distinctive visual language and the greatest continuity challenge.

**Good Mourning expansion completed:** [scene-by-scene treatments, six first-draft screenplays, and continuity ledger](../good-mourning/README.md). The original slate outlines remain the starting point for the other two projects.

**Original proposed next writing pass:** write a 10-page pilot, a scene-by-scene outline of the remaining fifty minutes, a dialogue-voice sheet, and a prop/wardrobe continuity ledger. Table-read the pilot with a stopwatch. These treatments establish dramatic causality; they do not replace dialogue drafts, clinical/technical consultation where relevant, animation tests, or a production budget.
