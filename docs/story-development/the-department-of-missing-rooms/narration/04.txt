The attendant found an exception. One room could be recovered as a family accommodation error. My sister's room. The other two would remain a capacity adjustment. I understood the offer perfectly. That was the worst part. There was a version of me who would have called it the best outcome available.

The bypass shared a housing with the air connections. Pulling everything would stop the purge and the breathing. We traced each line. Twice. Mrs. Rill made me point to where it ended, not where the drawing said it should end. This was a slower way to work. It was also the first useful thing I had learned in years.

The purge connection came free with a noise smaller than I expected. The pumps kept turning. Nobody applauded. We listened to the ordinary movement of air. I had thought courage would feel different from checking a valve. Mostly it felt like checking the valve again, because people were still on the other side.

The reset order arrived before my hands stopped shaking. It could restart the schedule, but not reconnect a cable. I sent the report outside my own account. Mrs. Rill checked that it had arrived. Evidence stored only where its author can delete it is sometimes less an account than a private arrangement with the truth.

We had stopped the deliberate danger. The temporary frames were still temporary. The seals were warming; the rooms needed to come out before the connections failed. I told the report that nine people remained inside. I said it again while we took our places. This time the number was not an argument for doing less.
