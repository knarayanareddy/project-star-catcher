# Small Worlds, Wrong Rules
## Two single-narrator cinematic mini-series

**Development and narration draft 1 · 6 November 2026**

Two independent series designed for the strengths of *Star Catcher*: compact episodes, one memorable voice, visual storytelling, evolving motifs, sound-led atmosphere, and an intimate emotional action inside a larger impossible world.

**Format choice:** six episodes per series, each targeting **4:00 including a 15-second end card**. Each season is 24 minutes. Four minutes sits inside the requested 3–5-minute range and gives the imagery room to carry the story. This episode count is a creative proposal, not a previously specified constraint.

| Series | Premise | Tone | Why one narrator matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| [The Department of Missing Rooms](../the-department-of-missing-rooms/series-bible.md) | A housing surveyor discovers that the city's promised efficiency comes from folding occupied rooms out of its official floor plans. His sister is inside one. | Bureaucratic sarcasm, architectural nightmare, dystopian rescue thriller. | His inspection recording changes from a tool of erasure into a precise account that makes the missing people impossible to dismiss. |
| [The Town That Forgot the Dark](../the-town-that-forgot-the-dark/series-bible.md) | A municipal bedtime reader discovers that the luminous moths powering her sleepless town need the darkness its leaders have abolished. Her familiar bedtime cadence has become a labor command. | Folk horror, industrial fairy tale, dreamlike ecology, tender gallows humor. | The same voice that trained the creatures to work must learn to stop commanding them and help the town tolerate silence. |

These are **new stories, not additions to the existing player** and not renamed versions of the ten-minute dialogue series. No second actor, character impersonation, or intelligible background speech is required. Other characters act through movement, decisions, objects, and expressions.

## What is supplied

Each series document contains:
- A complete season premise, ideology, emotional spine, and defined world rules.
- Narrator construction, silent supporting-character arcs, visual and sound language.
- All six episodes, each with five timed picture/sound beats and a complete first-draft narration passage for each beat.
- Setup/payoff and continuity checks, an actual ending, and practical production limits.

[Twelve narration-only scripts](narration-workflow.md) are also supplied as plain-text files for voice-generation tests. Each contains the five spoken passages for one episode, without production directions.

**Text marked VO is the spoken script. Everything else is production direction or story-bible material.** Do not feed the whole document to a speech generator. The narrator never reads episode headings, action directions, or the visible text on every prop.

## Timing and audio contract

Every episode uses this editorial template:

1. 00:00–00:45
2. 00:45–01:30
3. 01:30–02:15
4. 02:15–03:00
5. 03:00–03:45
6. 03:45–04:00: end card/credits

Each VO paragraph plays within its 45-second window, leaving the balance for pictures, pauses, and sound. Do not stretch a voice clip to fill the window. These are target allocations, not verified performance runtimes; a timed read and animatic must determine final pacing. No paragraph depends on an artificially slow reading to reach four minutes.

Generate with one stable voice identity per series, in separate beat-level files. Keep narration, music, ambience, and effects on separate tracks. Preserve approved recurring phrases by reusing actual recordings when the same recorded cue is heard again. Silent characters do not acquire dialogue just to explain the world. No literal singing is required.

## Tone and originality guardrails

- Horror comes from consequences and recognizable choices, not arbitrary rule changes.
- Sarcasm belongs to the narrator's defenses; it must occasionally fail when another person is at risk.
- Strange images must have a cause. Every impossible mechanism below has limits.
- These stories borrow *Star Catcher's* economy and tenderness, not its falling star, heart exchange, spinning top, monolith, or inverted ocean.
- Violence is mostly implied: compression danger, husks, pressure, darkness, and temporary bodily distortion. They do not need graphic gore to function.
- Working titles and names need clearance before release. These are first drafts, not production-locked scripts.

## Recommended first production

**The Department of Missing Rooms:** fewer creature assets, a strong opening hook, and a modular corridor that can support most of the season. Use the second series when ready for more organic animation and flock behavior.

**Next production step:** test one 45-second beat of each series with temporary narration, a simple animatic, and sound. Choose the voice and visual language before generating all twelve episodes.
