The first room returned one corner at a time. Wallpaper found the rest of its flowers. A door remembered how wide a person was. Three people came out. Mrs. Rill counted them, then made room for them to sit. It was the smallest room in the show apartment. It was no longer wasted space.

The second caught on a cable we had missed. I wanted to finish. Wanting was not a measurement. We stopped, cleared the bay, and started again. Three more people crossed the line. No one became braver by being hurried. The child carried the shoe I had seen in the shaft, and its ordinary size nearly undid me.

Lenna's release bar would not clear. Her table had fallen across it. I could see her shoulder against the wood. I could not reach her. For once there was no form I could put between myself and that fact. She passed the tape to another pair of hands and made space where she stood.

Two came out. Then my sister. I reached for her and she went back for someone's bag, because surviving does not immediately stop a person needing their things. I took the other handle. We carried it between us. Nine people. I will not say returned to normal. Normal had done this to them.

At five fifty-five we tagged the mechanism out of service. The attendant recorded the broken seal. I gave them my name without being asked. The morning purge had no rooms left to destroy. There would be consequences. This was the first accurate use of that word in a building that had been full of them all night.
