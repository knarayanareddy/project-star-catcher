# THE REPUBLIC OF TOMORROW
## A six-part limited series · 6 × 10 minutes

**Genre:** civic conspiracy thriller / deterministic satire.  
**Tagline:** You are free to make the approved choice.  
**Logline:** When a civic forecaster finds her own signature on the order that will seal her brother inside a flooding district, she has thirty-six hours to expose a safety model that manufactures its own certainty—and organize a real evacuation without the coercive machinery she has always trusted.

**The question:** Is preventing a predicted disaster still moral when the prevention policy produces the disaster?

## 1. The society and its ideological bargain

A low-lying coastal republic survived a catastrophic storm by pooling transport, reservoir, health, and energy data. Its **Civic Forecast Office** made infrastructure safer. Citizens remember the first winter when the lights stayed on. The Office earned trust before it demanded obedience.

Now a daily **Tomorrow Ledger** assigns the route, work slot, appointments, and shelter priority of every registered resident. The state guarantees necessities and promises near-zero preventable loss. People can appeal, but appeals take longer than the events they concern. “Freedom” means choosing among model-approved options.

The Office's charter requires exceptionally high confidence before compulsory detention or district sealing. Leadership solved uncertainty not by improving the model but by issuing movement orders that narrow the available outcomes. Official performance reports treat compliance-driven forecasts as independent validation.

Director Kade does not seek chaos, wealth, or godhood. He believes the population cannot coordinate fast enough without binding instructions. He has gradually made dissent itself a source of modeled danger.

### Everyday life

- A public crossing displays tomorrow's expected number of jaywalkers. An officer orders the last predicted walker across to close the day's variance.
- Citizens insure weddings against revised compatibility forecasts.
- Children practice “spontaneous assembly” in assigned positions. Joke and menace occupy the same frame.
- The informal economy consists of obsolete paper maps, conversations outside monitored channels, and people doing one another small unscheduled favors.
- Data gaps correlate with poverty: irregular shift workers and informal tenants are labeled unreliable, then denied the routes that would make their lives predictable.

## 2. Operational rules: no time travel

1. **The model is probabilistic, not prophetic.** Its reliable horizon is thirty-six hours. It uses weather, sensors, registries, and known behavior. It cannot observe future events or read private intentions.
2. **Public instructions alter the model's inputs.** If the Office closes a bridge, a forecast generated under that closure assumes the bridge is closed. Confusing conditional outputs with inevitable futures is the central abuse.
3. **The controversial confidence figure is not a death probability.** It is the model's estimate of how completely it can account for a district's movements under a specified policy. Leadership presents it as moral certainty about the safest action.
4. **Predictions have signed, time-stamped policy manifests.** Analysts can compare the same weather run under different orders. Public summaries hide those manifests; originals are mirrored at physical flood-control stations for emergency continuity.
5. **The flood is real.** An approaching surge will inundate the lower district through an overflow channel. The governing order seals its uphill tram corridor to protect central shelter capacity. Under that policy, residents remain exposed. Opening the corridor does not stop water; it creates an evacuation window.
6. **The central basin has finite capacity.** Evacuees need distributed shelter and counted transport. Simply opening a gate without coordination can create a crush. The ending must solve this material problem.
7. **An analog emergency protocol exists.** Paired local officers can transfer corridor control from central automation to a flood station for a fixed window. It was built for network failure; leadership has discouraged training, not removed the hardware.
8. **There are no parallel realities.** Alternate forecast images are clearly labeled simulations, with different policy headers. Memory, chronology, and causality stay stable.

### Relationship spine

Leena thinks Arun's anger proves he needs her protection; Arun thinks any assistance from her is another leash. Episode 2 establishes that she knowingly signed his earlier confinement. Episode 3 denies her the easy repair of a private exemption. Episode 4 requires her to disclose her own offense to the people she wants to lead. Episode 5 makes trust operational: he gives her a task she can fail, not blanket forgiveness. Episode 6 lets them work side by side without pretending their history disappeared.

## 4. Season causality and reveal design

A pending seal order carries Leena's auto-drafted authorization → she compares the forecast to the raw hazard → Nima finds identical crowd assets in supposedly independent simulations → signed manifests expose that the model assumes the seal → opening the corridor requires shelter and real counts → Leena gives up her personal exit to build a distributed evacuation → Rook invokes a pre-established local override → counted local coordination disproves the claim that binding central scripts are the only safe option.

**The signature is not from the future.** Standard procedure generates a draft order in Leena's name because she is the duty analyst scheduled to ratify it. She discovers it in episode 1 and can refuse it. The suspense is whether she will become the person the institution has planned for her to be.

**Clock:** approximately thirty-six hours, with explicit on-screen time remaining at each episode opening. No unexplained reset.

## 5. Six episodes

### Episode 1 — “As Expected”
**Time remaining:** 36 hours. **Objective:** verify a seal order before routine ratification. **Local outcome:** Leena withholds her signature and finds a policy-dependent forecast.

- **0:00–1:30 — Tomorrow as a product.** The crossing officer completes the predicted jaywalker tally. Upstairs, Leena corrects a cheerful forecast animation without noticing that the problem is the command, not the drawing.
- **1:30–3:30 — The personal exception.** Her duty queue contains an automated draft: flood cordon, Lower Nine, her signature field prefilled. Arun's address appears in the shelter exclusion list. Ratification is due in twelve hours; the order activates at the surge window.
- **3:30–5:30 — Rational reassurance.** Kade shows that opening central shelter doors indiscriminately could overwhelm their capacity. He offers Arun a staff-family exit permit. Leena takes it, ashamed but grateful.
- **5:30–7:45 — The discrepancy.** Nima asks why people in the forecast crowd turn toward a barrier three seconds before the barrier appears. Leena checks the run manifest: the barrier is an assumed instruction, not an observed event.
- **7:45–10:00 — First refusal.** Leena defers ratification and requests the unsealed scenario. Her access is suspended pending supervisory review. Nima saves a lawful working copy of the visible manifest. **Hook:** the next public bulletin calls Leena's “anticipated authorization” settled policy.

**Turn:** she sees how a scheduled decision is being laundered into inevitability. **Visual:** a signature box fills before her hand reaches the stylus.

### Episode 2 — “The Unreliable Citizen”
**Time remaining:** 29 hours. **Objective:** warn Arun and recover an independent copy of the policy runs. **Local outcome:** Arun refuses private escape; they locate the archived alternative.

- **0:00–1:30.** Leena enters Lower Nine on her staff permit. People are still working because the public bulletin describes the seal as a precaution with “no expected hardship.” Arun is repairing the uphill tram.
- **1:30–3:30.** She presents his exit permit. He asks where the other eleven crew permits are. Their argument reveals his earlier confinement and her signature. She cannot claim she was unaware of what signing means.
- **3:30–5:30.** Arun shows a physical flood-station map: the tram corridor rises above projected water. It remains usable for eighteen minutes after the surge begins, if people reach it in controlled groups. The danger is timing and crowd load, not an impassable landscape.
- **5:30–7:45.** Nima compares public renders. Reused figures alone prove nothing—simulation assets repeat—but their policy identifiers lead her to the station archive. She sends that concrete lead, not a miraculous inference.
- **7:45–10:00.** Rook refuses to surrender active control but allows a supervised archive inspection under Leena's still-valid professional identity. The earlier run projects a feasible evacuation with distributed shelters. **Hook:** Kade rejected it because unregistered residents made the headcount uncertain.

**Turn:** Leena's personal rescue is exposed as another form of unequal allocation. Arun gains information, not a reason to trust her yet.

### Episode 3 — “A Very High Confidence”
**Time remaining:** 21 hours. **Objective:** prove what the confidence number actually measures. **Local outcome:** they have legible evidence but no executable evacuation plan.

- **0:00–1:30.** Rook demonstrates the paired emergency keys during an equipment check. She names the limits: local control expires after eighteen minutes, load sensors remain active, two credentialed officers must co-sign. Arun has a maintenance credential; Leena a policy credential. The finale's tool is now explicit.
- **1:30–3:30.** The team runs the same hazard under sealed and open policies at the archive terminal. The official high-confidence scenario “accounts for” confined residents by keeping them in place. Its certainty is about compliance, not survival.
- **3:30–5:30.** Kade explains his choice over a station call. An uncertain count might overwhelm shelters. Leena asks why uncertainty was not disclosed to the residents. He replies that disclosure would change behavior and invalidate the model. His circular logic becomes visible.
- **5:30–7:45.** Leena releases the two manifests with a plain-language annotation. People see the problem—but the bulletin alone causes conflicting departures. A queue blocks a service lane; no one is killed, but Rook must clear it.
- **7:45–10:00.** Arun tells Leena that proof is not transport. They inventory actual trams, beds, and stewards. **Hook:** central automation has canceled the next day's uphill service because the sealed forecast assumes nobody will need it.

**Turn:** exposure without preparation is shown as inadequate. The heroes inherit a practical obligation.

### Episode 4 — “Please Remain Spontaneous”
**Time remaining:** 12 hours. **Objective:** assemble a counted route and shelter network before central sealing. **Local outcome:** the network works at test scale; Leena sacrifices her privileged exit.

- **0:00–1:30.** Residents ignore an official anti-rumor broadcast filmed in front of a visibly dry rehearsal tank. The humor comes from over-produced certainty, not from mocking disaster victims.
- **1:30–3:30.** Arun recruits tram stewards from his maintenance crew. Nima puts capacity and departure windows on plain paper, with blank boxes for actual arrivals. Three upland buildings offer verified floor space. Rook insists on an accessible boarding route and assisted-transport roster.
- **3:30–5:30.** A building refuses participation because Leena signed its organizer's confinement. She admits what she did and provides her records for a later claim. Suri-like universal forgiveness is not imported here: the building agrees because its residents need a route, not because she makes a good speech.
- **5:30–7:45.** A twenty-person route drill reveals a jam at a narrow turn. They alter boarding groups and remove an ornamental forecast screen obstructing the ramp. Real observation improves the plan more than another polished projection.
- **7:45–10:00.** Kade offers Leena reinstatement and an exit shuttle for both siblings if she authenticates the central seal. She gives the shuttle slot to an assisted-transport group under the permit's capacity allowance and declines reinstatement. **Hook:** Kade assigns another analyst to ratify the order; refusing to sign has not stopped the machinery.

**Turn:** Leena loses the fantasy that her individual refusal is enough. The alternative must work independently of her authority.

### Episode 5 — “No Expected Casualties”
**Time remaining:** 40 minutes. **Objective:** transfer corridor control legally and physically before the seal traps the remaining residents. **Local outcome:** local control begins, but the real count exceeds the official model.

- **0:00–1:30.** The substitute analyst's order activates. The flood is arriving. Do not intercut with apocalyptic spectacle elsewhere: stay with locks, rising water marks, and people who need to move.
- **1:30–3:30.** Rook requires verified capacity before invoking the override. Nima's linked shelter sheets supply it. Rook records the transfer and announces her name and liability. This is not a hacked keypad or a stirring speech that changes physics.
- **3:30–5:30.** Arun and Leena turn their paired keys at the local station; the corridor reopens for eighteen minutes under sensor-limited loading. The first groups move. Shelters send back arrival counts by radio.
- **5:30–7:45.** The official roster undercounts informal tenants by sixty-three people. Kade urges resealing before the corridor saturates. Leena refuses to “correct” the tally downward. Nima places the actual discrepancy on the public feed beside the model's confidence number.
- **7:45–10:00.** Arun diverts one tram to collect the missing residents through a service platform established in episode 2. This requires clearing shelter capacity from a government briefing hall. **Hook:** Kade is the only official currently able to authorize access to that hall.

**Turn:** uncertainty now has names and bodies. Kade must choose between defending his forecast and contributing a specific resource.

### Episode 6 — “Observed”
**Time remaining:** 11 minutes of the local-control window. **Objective:** complete the evacuation without falsifying capacity or abandoning the unregistered group. **Local outcome:** the corridor is cleared; the event becomes a documented limit on central authority.

- **0:00–1:30.** Kade unlocks the briefing hall and sends its usable capacity, not another certainty claim. He has not converted to an anarchist; he accepts evidence that his current policy is failing.
- **1:30–3:30.** Arun's tram reaches the service platform. Nima and local stewards match arrivals to spaces. A radio fails; paper numbers travel with runners. The supposedly inefficient backup works because people rehearsed it.
- **3:30–5:30.** A platform load alarm stops boarding. Leena does not override the sensor for dramatic heroism. Arun clears the jammed empty carrier, restoring the measured safe load. Technical competence matters, and the system's useful safeguards remain useful.
- **5:30–7:45.** The last group crosses before the corridor floods. The flood takes the vacated platform, not a surprise sacrificial protagonist. The team publishes signed headcounts and known uncertainties; they do not announce that every possible risk has been abolished.
- **7:45–10:00.** Six weeks later, an inquiry suspends compulsory forecast-based confinements pending review and places district seals under dual local authorization. Kade testifies. Leena's past detention orders are included in the investigation. Arun shares a workbench with her, not an absolution speech. The new bulletin reads: “Surge probability: 38–51%. Decision meeting: 18:00.” **Final image:** a child asks whether that means it will rain. Leena says, “We don't know yet.” They take an umbrella and go out.

**Closed:** Lower Nine's entrapment, the inflated certainty metric, unilateral corridor control, the missing roster. **Open:** how to govern risk fairly when prediction remains useful and uncertainty cannot be eliminated.

## 7. Logic stress test

- **Why not destroy the model?** Weather forecasting, load sensors, and infrastructure coordination save lives. Destroying them would worsen the storm. The target is compulsory policy disguised as objective prediction.
- **Why doesn't Kade arrest everyone?** His order remains legal until challenged; widespread arrests would remove essential tram and flood staff during a documented emergency. He uses credential pressure, replacement analysts, and narrow access control instead. He is powerful, not omnipotent.
- **Why are archives accessible?** Flood stations need policy history offline if the central network fails. Leena has professional archive rights before she loses operational privileges; Rook supervises access. Evidence is not obtained by a convenient universal password.
- **Why does the route have only eighteen minutes?** Surveyed elevation and the surge curve determine the window. The number is established before the climax and never extends because a character is sympathetic.
- **Why doesn't reopening doom the center?** Distributed shelter capacity and measured boarding make it feasible. Before those exist, Kade's capacity objection is genuinely serious.
- **Why is the ending not total victory?** One successful evacuation and exposed reporting fraud justify interim limits and investigation, not instant consensus about all governance.

## 8. Audiovisual and production bible

**Hero prop:** a folded paper tram map. Initially a discredited relic, then a shared work surface covered with actual counts. The map does not become correct through nostalgia: it must be checked against present infrastructure.

**Palette:** official white/blue with smooth forecast amber; maintenance spaces use wet concrete, warning red, and visible tool colors. Simulations always carry a border and policy header so the audience is never tricked about whether a death actually happened.

**Sound:** daily bulletin chimes become a countdown. The score's steady pulse loses beats when actual counts diverge. In the last crossing, use radio replies, wheel noise, and breathing instead of nonstop orchestral insistence.

**Locations:** forecast office, tram workshop/platform, flood-control station, lower-district corridor, upland hall. Reuse a physical corridor with waterline and lighting changes. Reserve the largest effects shot for the empty platform disappearing beneath water after evacuation; scale follows a completed moral choice.

**Sample voice exchange:**

> KADE: The model has accounted for every registered resident.  
> ARUN: Congratulations. I live next to four people who don't exist.  
> LEENA: They exist.  
> ARUN: Then stop letting him finish that sentence.

**What to resist:** making random behavior the heroic cure for determinism. An evacuation succeeds through more accountable coordination, not because someone flips a coin.
