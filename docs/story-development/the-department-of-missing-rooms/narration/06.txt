By breakfast, the Department had prepared a successful recovery announcement. I was offered a sentence about swift corrective action. The sentence contained no people who had needed correcting, which was an improvement on the previous plan. Mrs. Rill brought the report. I left the recorder running while the banner came down.

We recorded nine names, checked by the people who owned them. Lenna corrected a letter in hers. I had been about to tell her it was close enough. The reflex survived the night. I mention that because this is not a report about becoming a different man. It is about finally noticing what the old one does.

Three weeks later, my certification was under review. The three rooms remained open under a safety order. Other buildings still folded. I cannot honestly finish with the city waking up. What happened here had become harder to deny. That is smaller than justice. It is larger than the private exception I nearly accepted.

Lenna did not ask me to narrate what I had learned. She asked me to hold the curtain rail straight. Then she gave me a chair. I sat down without inspecting it. We had tea beside a wall that stayed where it was. I had no useful information to contribute. She seemed pleased by this.

Final inventory. Three rooms recovered. Nine occupants accounted for. One official signature referred for investigation: mine. The cloth tape is still short by an inch. We mark the difference rather than pretending it is perfect. There is room in the margin. There always was. I am learning who the margin was meant to hold.
