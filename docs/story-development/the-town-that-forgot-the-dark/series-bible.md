# THE TOWN THAT FORGOT THE DARK
## Six episodes × 4:00 · one narrator · 24-minute complete season

**Tagline:** Sleep well. Your dreams are on the clock.

**Logline:** A town's beloved bedtime reader discovers that its dream-fed moths are keeping thirty-one people asleep to maintain the promise of uninterrupted light. To bring them home, she must stop reading the work cycle she taught the colony and help her neighbors survive the one hour of darkness their town has been trained to fear.

**Genre balance:** a comforting bedtime voice inside a folk-horror ecology; industrial dystopia with restrained gallows humor; bodily and visual strangeness governed by a single fictional species, not an unrestricted dream universe.

**Theme:** a society that calls every pause a failure will eventually turn rest into another kind of work.

## 1. The town's bargain

The town of **Lumen** once had ordinary nights and recurring winter blackouts. It learned to cultivate luminous **lantern moths**: a fictional species whose light organs brighten while feeding on the patterned neural activity of sleeping humans. This is the story's speculative premise, not a real claim about dreams or energy.

A regulated exchange originally kept a small settlement lit through storms. Volunteers slept during feeding sessions; the moths received dark rest, and the humans woke. The moths were trained with the municipal reader's three-part cadence to gather, feed, and return to the roost. The reader became a comforting civic figure.

After a devastating storm, the Lighting Board suspended the moths' rest hour for two nights to keep pumps running. Emergency production became a permanent target. The town grew around the promise of continuous light. The suspended rest has now lasted twenty-eight days.

A collector system draws light from the colony and keeps its rearing chambers stimulated while connected. Exhausted moths cling to feeding sleepers, creating a dangerous feedback cycle: more promised power requires longer feeding, which creates more exhausted moths and more sleepers who do not wake. The Board labels these people **reserve sleepers**.

The sincere social good is reliable power. The abuse is hiding who supplies it and treating ecological limits as disloyalty. The aim is not to abolish electricity or romanticize dangerous darkness.

## 2. Sole narrator and silent characters

### Iona Senn, 64 — municipal bedtime reader

Iona has read Lumen to sleep for thirty-five years. Her warm voice is recognizable before her face is. She considers her work ordinary public service and distrusts grand pronouncements, except the comforting ones she has made a career of delivering.

- **Want:** finish her final week, train a replacement, and believe she has done a gentle thing.
- **Wound:** her husband died years earlier during a winter outage. He stays dead; he does not appear as a moth, dream apparition, or hidden recording.
- **False belief:** keeping everyone calm is the same as keeping everyone safe.
- **Need:** tell the truth without replacing a useful ritual with a new command, and accept limits she cannot narrate away.
- **Narrative frame:** an overnight maintenance diary addressed to the next reader, not a literal omniscient fairy-tale narrator. A clearly marked one-week follow-up closes the account.
- **Voice trajectory:** affectionate municipal bedtime ritual → dry discrepancies → horrified self-recognition → calm, specific emergency instructions → a private voice that no longer needs the town to obey.

### Teo Marr, 23 — apprentice reader and night electrician

Teo fixes the equipment behind Iona's supposedly effortless ritual. He has requested a moth rest interval repeatedly; his marked rosters were filed as training notes. He volunteered for an extended feeding session to protect an older coworker and is now one of the thirty-one who cannot wake normally. His repair diagrams, spare headset, and planned shutdown sequence remain usable work, not cryptic messages from a perfect martyr.

He is visibly alive. His recovery is gradual; the ending does not install him as a new permanent bedtime authority.

### Keeper Harl, 56 — colony supervisor

Remembers the storm that justified suspension. Has maintained production by telling himself every extension is the last. He first blocks access, then presents permanent automated readings as a retirement gift, then must choose a concrete task in a town-wide shutdown. Helping does not erase his falsified reports. No voiced confession is necessary: the original rosters, signed extensions, and his physical choices show his role.

### The window-makers' crew

Three recurring silent workers who maintain the nursery shutters, inspect backup circuits, and help guide the colony. Their preparation in episode 4 makes the hour possible. They are not a surprise rebel army arriving in the finale.

### The thirty-one sleepers

Do not imply that all are permanently disabled, dead, or possessed. Each is alive but entrained in extended feeding. Sleepers vary in age and appearance; the visual count and follow-up chart must remain consistent. Other townspeople sleep ordinarily without being connected to the feeding system.

## 3. Fixed rules

1. **Moths are organisms, not dead people.** They mimic imagery from the dreams they feed on. A human-shaped projection is copied content, not a trapped soul requiring mystical rescue.
2. **One uninterrupted hour of dark rest per twenty-four hours is essential to colony health.** This requirement appears in the nursery handbook before the climax. A few seconds of lights-out cannot reset it.
3. **Feeding is physical and limited.** Moths feed through fine, removable filaments at supervised sleeper stations. They do not harvest every unconscious person in town at unlimited distance.
4. **The collector maintains stimulation.** Feeding valves must be closed and the collector disconnected before the colony can settle. Darkening the public screens, closing a curtain, or merely saying something soothing is insufficient. The nursery has shutters to keep stray light out once the collector is isolated.
5. **Forced removal is unsafe.** At high stimulation the filaments tighten. Shut feeding, remove stimulation, guide the colony to roost, then allow filaments to release. The emergency plan states this order in episode 2.
6. **Iona's cadence is learned behavior.** The three established cue words through the feeding PA guide gather/feed/return. Her ordinary diary narration is recorded on a separate handheld recorder and does not issue commands. Her voice has no general magical power.
7. **The old cycle's return cue has no safe destination while the nursery remains stimulated.** Repeating the loop drives continued feeding rather than genuine rest. Changing one word does not bypass the physical problem.
8. **The town loses main generation during rest.** Tested backup cells can support pumps, clinic equipment, and safety markers for seventy-five minutes. They cannot also support decorative lights, factory shifts, and all household use. No infinite battery appears in episode 5.
9. **Darkness is not risk-free.** Streets close to traffic, hazards are marked, and essential care stays powered. People may use shielded emergency lights away from the roost; nobody is required to stumble in total darkness for ideological purity.
10. **The colony may release copied dream images as it sheds exhausted scales.** These images cannot predict events, unlock doors, resurrect anyone, or change physical geography. They dissolve with the scales; the town remains materially the same place.
11. **Waking is uneven.** Ending the extraction permits recovery, not instant restoration of all thirty-one at the same second. Some need ongoing observation. Iona cannot promise their outcomes.
12. **One intelligible voice only.** The PA cue uses Iona's own approved recording. No singing, chorus, whispered children, radio announcer, or alternate character voice is required. Nonverbal creature sounds and breaths are permitted, never hidden dialogue.

## 4. Design language

**Hero object:** a black velvet nursery hood, originally used to cover resting moths. Iona first mistakes it for a ceremonial garment. Its real purpose is a small demonstration of the larger neglected need.

**Visual motifs:** chalk sleep marks; windows that resemble pinned specimens; pale dust shaped briefly like household objects; Teo's empty headset; three evenly spaced chalk strokes, later left unfinished.

**Creature design:** soft, domestic-scale moths first; exhausted wing membranes carry unnervingly precise impressions of human teeth, staircases, or half a face. These are light patterns, not people literally growing into insects. The nursery's scale supplies awe; no giant final queen is added to invent a new boss fight.

**Palette:** butter-yellow municipal comfort; moth silver; deep indigo rest. Darkness reveals shapes and distance rather than simply making the screen black for a minute.

**Sound:** ventilation, wing friction, electrical ticking. Iona's short three-cue recording is initially reassuring and later unbearable. The sound of the collector stopping should expose—not eliminate—ordinary room noise.

**Narrator casting:** mature feminine warmth, conversational dry wit. No constant sinister delivery. The capacity to sound safe is precisely what makes Iona useful to the institution and moving when she tells the truth.

## 5. Complete episodes: picture beats and spoken narration

Read only **VO**. Every beat is 45 seconds, with deliberate space after the spoken paragraph. Each episode reserves 03:45–04:00 for its end card. This is a visual series: a sound-only edition needs a separate adaptation.

### Episode 1 — “Good Night, Productive Citizens”

**Local objective:** complete the nightly read and find the absent apprentice. **Change:** Iona discovers that a successful sleep count includes people who have not woken. **Clock:** 21:00.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** Lumen glows under a night sky with no visible stars because of light spill, not because the stars are fake. Curtains close in sequence. Iona starts the approved three-cue recording in the control booth, then speaks privately into her diary recorder.

**VO:** In Lumen, we have excellent sleep figures. Ninety-eight percent settle before ten. The other two percent maintain the figures. For thirty-five years, I have been the voice that tells the town it is safe to close its eyes. Tonight I was meant to teach my replacement. His chair was empty. The bedtime was not.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Teo's headset rests on a repaired switchboard. Iona finds his signed extended-feeding form and an unanswered maintenance request. Through a window, a clinic lamp runs on a labeled backup cell.

**VO:** Teo fixed the microphone that made my voice sound effortless. He had left his tools in order, which was how I knew he had not intended to leave. The Board said he was contributing additional rest. I had read that phrase often. It became less comfortable when I could see the outline his hands had worn into the desk.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** Harl escorts Iona into the nursery. Rows of sleeper stations are lit gently, each with a feeding filament. The count is thirty-one. Iona reaches toward Teo, then sees the filaments tighten under bright collector light.

**VO:** There were thirty-one beds behind the nursery glass. Every sleeper had volunteered for a shift. None had volunteered for the extra days. Teo's chest rose. A thread of light connected his wrist to a moth above him. I reached for it, and the thread pulled tight. Even help can become another kind of tugging if you don't know what is holding on.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** A moth's wing carries a precise image of Teo's workbench, then an impossible little staircase. It settles on Iona's hand. The image slides off as dust and vanishes on the floor.

**VO:** The moth opened its wings. There was Teo's workbench, small enough to cover with my thumb. Then a staircase to a room he had never built. They feed on dreaming. We know this. We print it on the school tours. We do not usually ask what happens when the dreaming cannot stop.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** Iona finds the black velvet hood beneath an old handbook. Its rest schedule reads one hour in every twenty-four. The current rosters show twenty-eight consecutive rest cancellations. Harl turns the top page over; she turns it back.

**VO:** The old keeper's hood was not for ceremony. It was for darkness. One hour, every day, without interruption. Twenty-eight crossed-out hours lay under Harl's hand. I had thought we were borrowing a little light from the night. Apparently we had decided the night was not entitled to have any of its own.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. A moth shelters beneath the velvet edge.

### Episode 2 — “Additional Rest”

**Local objective:** learn how to end feeding without injuring the sleepers. **Change:** Iona discovers the command hidden inside her comfort ritual. **Clock:** 22:00–23:00.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** Harl shows the storm photograph and original two-night emergency suspension. The same approval stamp repeats on twenty-six more days. Ordinary pump equipment occupies the background, making the original need concrete.

**VO:** The first suspension lasted two nights. A storm had flooded the lower pumps. People would have died without power. Harl kept the original order where everyone could see it. The extensions were underneath. A good reason is a useful place to hide a bad habit. It continues looking brave long after the emergency has changed its address.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Iona reads Teo's annotated emergency sequence: close feeding valves, isolate collector, guide return, lower shutters, uninterrupted sixty-minute rest. Beside it, backup cells labeled seventy-five minutes under essential-load testing. The general lighting load is explicitly excluded.

**VO:** Teo had written a shutdown plan. Feeding first. Collector second. Return cue. Shutters. One complete hour. The backup cells could carry the clinic and pumps for seventy-five minutes, not the factories and decorative towers as well. This was not a choice between light and death. It was a choice about what we were willing to stop doing.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** Iona plays her familiar cue into the feeding PA: the same three words, Rest. Rise. Return. Moths gather, brighten, circle, then attach again because the nursery collector remains stimulated. She stops the recording.

**VO:** Rest. Rise. Return. Three little words I had made soft enough for children. To the moths they meant gather, feed, and go home. But home was still lit. So they circled back to the sleepers. My voice was not comforting a town. It was operating a shift that no longer had an end.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** Iona passes a larger hood over a small, labeled reserve-bank chamber, disconnected from the collector, before putting the small hood over a separate test chamber containing a single moth. The reserve bank remains resting throughout preparation. The moth settles. Its wing markings fade. She lifts the edge too soon; the moth startles. She replaces it and leaves it alone.

**VO:** Under the hood, one moth stopped moving. I lifted the edge to make sure it was all right. It startled, and I put the darkness back. I have reassured thousands of people by checking on them. There are occasions when checking is simply another way of refusing to let a thing rest.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** Iona compares Teo's extended-feeding form with Harl's subsequent renewals. Teo's signature is present only on the first. She photographs the sequence with the diary's timecode. Harl places a prepared authorization for permanent automated readings beside Iona's retirement badge: her voice would continue working after she leaves.

**VO:** Teo had signed for one shift. The extensions carried the keeper's mark. Beside them lay my retirement form: permission to run my recorded voice forever. They were offering to let me rest by making sure the part of me they needed never could. I left the form unsigned.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. The permanent-reading authorization remains unsigned.

### Episode 3 — “The Things We Dream for Free”

**Local objective:** stop the cycle being restarted and make the danger visible. **Change:** a private discovery becomes a public service problem. **Clock:** 00:00–01:00.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** Iona removes the loop cartridge and labels it DO NOT RESTART. Harl points to a duplicate in the reserve drawer. She places that on the table too. Neither is destroyed; the issue is control, not a magical cursed recording.

**VO:** I stopped the loop. Harl showed me the spare. Of course there was a spare. We had planned carefully for the possibility that comfort might fail to arrive on time. We had not planned for the possibility that it might keep arriving after someone no longer wanted it. I put both recordings where we could see them.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Across the nursery, wings flicker with borrowed domestic images: a kettle, a running dog, a door left open. Dust carries brief silhouettes through the corridor. None touches or changes a physical object. Iona's own dead husband does not appear.

**VO:** Exhausted scales came away from the wings. For a moment the corridor filled with things people had dreamed: a dog, an open door, someone standing at the wrong end of a childhood table. They were not visitors. They were impressions. I say this because grief is very willing to mistake resemblance for permission to believe.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** Iona approaches the broadcast microphone, sets down a familiar reassurance script, and records a different announcement. Residents see her live image and the sleeper count. The diary waveform and public PA indicators are visually distinct.

**VO:** I told the town there were thirty-one people in extended feeding. I did not say they were peacefully resting. I did not promise they would all wake the moment we did the right thing. I said the light had a cost we had stopped reporting. It was the least soothing announcement of my career.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** Residents react without audible speech: a clinic worker points to powered equipment, a cyclist points to an unlit route, a factory foreman to a running machine. Iona writes these needs on a physical load board. Not every objection is selfish.

**VO:** People were frightened. Some were angry. They brought lists. Machines that could not simply stop, journeys that still had to happen, a relative whose breathing depended on a socket. Their questions were not proof that they loved the system. They were the work required if I meant what I had just asked them to consider.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** Three window-makers arrive with Teo's marked shutdown diagram and shutter tools. Iona gives them the plan rather than taking their tools. Harl watches a full essentials list replace the production target on the board.

**VO:** The window-makers had copies of Teo's notes. He had not been waiting for one important person to understand him. He had been doing the work with other people. They started checking the shutters. I found myself standing in the way, holding a microphone. For once I could improve the situation by moving aside.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. Tools arranged beside a deliberately incomplete chalk cycle.

### Episode 4 — “A Rehearsal for Night”

**Local objective:** prepare a safe hour without main generation. **Change:** Harl must contribute resources rather than another promise; Iona stops performing omniscience. **Clock:** 02:00–03:30.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** The window-makers test nursery shutters, discover a warped joint, and repair it. A beam leaks through, then disappears. Iona holds a work lamp at their direction, not toward the resting test moth.

**VO:** The first shutter did not close. This was fortunate to discover before we needed it closed. There is a kind of hope that arrives holding a screwdriver. I recommend it over the sort that requires everyone to stop mentioning the hole. We patched the joint and tested it again. The dark stayed where we put it.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** An essential-load test runs on backup: clinic, pumps, marked walkways. A decorative tower is deliberately disconnected. Load indicators remain within their documented test range. No medically essential machine is experimentally cut off.

**VO:** We transferred the essential circuits with the clinic staff watching their equipment. The decorative tower went dark. Nothing dreadful happened to the tower. It remained decorative. The pumps held. The cells held. We wrote down what we had tested instead of announcing that we were ready for anything. I had become suspicious of that particular sentence.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** Residents mark step edges, close a traffic gate, check doors, and guide an older neighbor to a safe room. Iona looks at the storm memorial. She pauses there, then rejoins the practical work.

**VO:** My husband died in a winter outage. For years, any darkness felt like agreement with what had taken him. But that night had no warning, no backup, no one at the lower stairs. We were making those things now. Preparing for the dark was not the same as welcoming harm. I wished I had learned that before I had a voice people trusted.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** Harl receives an order to resume full output. He looks at his falsified rosters. He signs the emergency isolation log instead, gives the window-makers the collector key, and personally checks all thirty-one feeding valves with Iona observing.

**VO:** Harl signed the isolation record. It did not unsign the extensions. He gave us the collector key and took a place at the feeding valves. I did not forgive him. He did not ask me to. This was a better arrangement than either of us making a speech about difficult decisions while someone else turned the handles.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** The schedule assigns 03:55 to feeding isolation and collector shutdown, then 04:00 to closed-shutter rest, with 05:00 completion. Seventy-five minutes of tested reserve; fifteen minutes contingency. All thirty-one station numbers are accounted for. Iona removes the reassurance script from the microphone stand.

**VO:** We scheduled the hour before sunrise, with fifteen minutes of reserve beyond it. Thirty-one valves. One collector. Shutters checked. Routes marked. I could not promise the sleepers would wake safely. I could promise not to hide them inside a better-sounding number. It was a smaller thing to say. It was finally something I could keep.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. 03:54. The cue cartridge is beside the switch, not in it.

### Episode 5 — “One Hour”

**Local objective:** complete the real dark-rest interval without breaking essential care or restarting the extraction. **Change:** the old comfort voice becomes a final bounded instruction, then silence. **Story clock:** 03:55–05:00, deliberately compressed within four minutes. The full rest hour starts only when the colony is inside and the shutters close at 04:00.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** 03:55. Harl and crew close all feeding valves; Iona checks the numbered board. Collector isolator opens. Pumps and clinic equipment remain powered on separate backup. The main civic glow fades, revealing distant unpolluted sky.

**VO:** At three fifty-five, the feeding valves closed. Then the collector stopped. We did it in that order. I am leaving the order in this account because beautiful stories have a habit of forgetting the instructions that kept everyone alive. The town went dim. The clinic did not. Somewhere a child discovered the window could show more than its own reflection.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** Iona speaks the return cue once, in its trained cadence, through the feeding PA. Moths detach gradually and follow the established guide path into the darkened nursery roost. At 04:00 all moths are inside and workers close the shutters; only now does the one-hour rest timer start. Iona switches the PA off and returns to her handheld recorder.

**VO:** I gave the return cue once. With the collector quiet, home was finally somewhere they could go. The filaments loosened. The moths gathered along the guide rail and passed into the nursery. At four we closed the shutters and began the hour. Then I switched off the speaker. I had no fourth word to offer. That was the point of the hour.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** 04:17. Teo's eyelids move but do not open. Outside, someone starts to reconnect an ornamental sign, sees the essentials list, and puts the plug down. Backup indicators stay inside safe limits. Iona sits rather than pacing beside Teo's bed.

**VO:** Seventeen minutes felt long enough to doubt everything. Teo did not wake. The old reflex offered me a remedy: put the voice back, put the light back, make the room look as though we had done something. We had done something. Now we had to avoid undoing it because waiting looked unimpressive.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** 04:43. A shutter latch rattles loose. A window-maker secures it from outside without opening the shutter. A sliver of light reaches only the outer vestibule, never the closed inner roost. Iona watches, keeping the command loop off.

**VO:** A latch slipped. We caught the outer shutter before the inner chamber was exposed. No miracle held it. A person with a tired arm did. We changed places when the arm shook. This is the part of a night people rarely put on monuments: taking over a small necessary task before someone else can no longer manage it.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** 05:00. The full hour completes. Workers leave the roost dark and restart only a reduced collector bank outside its resting section, using a rested reserve group established in the nursery layout. Teo opens his eyes; other beds remain under observation. Iona does not reach for the microphone.

**VO:** At five, the hour was complete. We brought one rested bank online and kept the exhausted colony quiet. Teo opened his eyes. I will not improve the moment by saying everyone did. Some needed longer. The first thing he looked for was his headset. I put it out of reach, and then asked myself whether I had learned anything. So I put it on the table and waited.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. Teo sees the headset and leaves it where it is.

### Episode 6 — “The Unread Page”

**Local objective:** prevent recovery from becoming a new production target. **Change:** Iona leaves behind a practice with limits instead of another permanent command. **Time:** morning, then one week later.

#### 00:00–00:45
**Picture/sound:** A new chart lists each sleeper's status individually. Teo drinks water with help; two others are awake, several stir, others remain observed. Harl enters the unedited chart into the public ledger beside the cancellation history.

**VO:** In the morning, we listed people rather than percentages. Awake. Resting. Still being watched. Every line had a name beside it. Harl entered the canceled rest hours with his own mark attached. We had not earned the right to call this a success story before the people inside it had finished needing care.

#### 00:45–01:30
**Picture/sound:** ONE WEEK LATER. All thirty-one have woken, with some still recuperating under care. A group of residents verifies the log. Harl's key is held under dual custody pending review. The production chart now visibly includes rotating moth banks and protected rest.

**VO:** A week later, all thirty-one had woken. Some were still too tired to go home. The keeper's decisions were under review. Two people now had to authorize an extension, and nobody could authorize canceling the colony's rest. These were local rules, not a new human nature. They would need people willing to notice when the ink stopped meaning anything.

#### 01:30–02:15
**Picture/sound:** Workshops run shorter shifts. A bakery posts later hours; a resident is irritated, another relieved. Essential circuits retain tested reserves. Nursery moth banks rotate between feeding and dark rest; less light is available for decorative streets.

**VO:** We had less light to waste. Some work took longer. The bakery opened later and produced bread of no obvious ideological difference. People complained. I found this reassuring. They had begun complaining about actual inconveniences instead of being told that any inconvenience meant the town might die. We were learning the difference between a limit and a threat.

#### 02:15–03:00
**Picture/sound:** Iona and Teo sit at the reader's desk. He edits the handbook to separate human bedtime announcements from moth work cues. He chooses a short daytime reading session, not overnight extraction duty. She hands him the book but keeps neither his headset nor his decision.

**VO:** Teo did not become the new voice of bedtime. Not immediately. He wanted to repair the training system first. We separated the human invitation to rest from the moths' work signals. It was embarrassing how long we had called those the same thing. I gave him the handbook. I did not tell him which voice to use.

#### 03:00–03:45
**Picture/sound:** Iona closes her maintenance diary. At the scheduled quiet interval, the public speaker remains off. A moth settles beneath the velvet hood in the nursery; outside, safely lit doorways lead toward a genuinely dark patch of sky. She looks up without a final supernatural reveal.

**VO:** I left one page unread. Not a warning. Not the missing answer. A space at the end of the day when nobody had to be instructed how to feel about it. The town rested. The moths rested. I was still awake, listening to a place that no longer needed me to say it was peaceful before it could become quiet.

**End card:** 03:45–04:00. Ordinary night insects, distant water pipes, no return of the work loop.

### Continuity clarifications

- **Rested reserve bank:** episode 2 explicitly shows a small disconnected reserve chamber under velvet, containing a group separate from the exhausted working colony. That bank receives normal rest during preparation. Its limited output is why only reduced generation returns at 05:00. It cannot power the town's previous demand or eliminate the need for the blackout. This is a required early picture detail, not an optional late explanation.
- **Full hour stays full:** the feeding isolation starts at 03:55; all moths are inside and shutters close at 04:00. The complete 04:00–05:00 dark interval is story time, not sixty minutes of screen time. Use the explicit 04:17 and 04:43 clocks. No stray light enters the closed inner roost; otherwise the uninterrupted-hour rule would be broken.
- **Who is asleep?** Thirty-one connected volunteers are stuck in extended feeding. Many ordinary residents are awake during the emergency; the town is not universally hypnotized by the reader.
- **Why doesn't anyone remove the moths immediately?** High-stimulation filaments tighten when pulled, established on Teo in 1. The safe sequence is identified in 2 and followed in 5.
- **Why not just black out the nursery and keep normal generation?** The collector's stimulation circuit and active feeding must be isolated; full normal output depends on that working colony. Backup and the small reserve bank cannot meet normal load. Shutters alone do not stop extraction.
- **Is Iona omniscient?** No. She sees the rosters, speaks her own memories, records the public response, and states the week-later outcome only after the time jump. She never narrates Teo's private dream as fact.
- **Harl's responsibility:** public records and key restrictions remain. One useful action does not make him the benevolent sole keeper of the new order.
- **What changes materially?** Protected rest, reduced output, tested essential reserves, separated cues, transparent sleeper logs. The ending does not claim no future storms or no need for labor.

## 7. Production limits

Five reusable environments: reading booth, nursery/feeding station, shutter walkway, one civic street, small clinic observation area. Start with a handful of individually animated moths; imply the colony through layered silhouettes, localized light, and wing sound. No city-destruction sequence is required.

The most expensive image is the shared dream-scale corridor in episode 3. Keep it short, using a few sharply recognizable household shapes rather than an unlimited parade of visual ideas. Its purpose is to reveal extraction, not pause the plot for a showreel.

One mature narrator, one reused cue recording in that same voice. Spoken diary and PA are distinguished by microphone treatment, not by hiring another actor. Breath, wing movement, and mechanical sound may remain; no intelligible crowd dialogue.

**Content boundary:** disturbing wings, prolonged sleep, bodily filaments, and institutional endangerment. Avoid fetishized restraint or graphic invasive procedures. The technology and organisms are fictional, not an allegory that equates real sleep disorders or clinical treatment with moral failure.
