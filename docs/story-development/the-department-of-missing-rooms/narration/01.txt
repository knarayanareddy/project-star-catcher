Our building became three rooms more efficient on a Thursday. Nobody moved out. This was considered an improvement. I was sent to verify the improvement, because the city allows a discrepancy of two rooms before requiring a person. I am that person. My name is Orrin Voss. I used to find the distinction amusing.

My sister's sewing room was between the laundry and the stairs. I knew because I had carried her machine up both flights and mentioned it every birthday since. The new plan placed the laundry beside the stairs. I stood in the useful space where my sister was no longer supposed to be.

Folding was invented for emergencies. Fire damage. Flooding. Repairs. A room waits in storage while its place is made safe. According to the notice, this place had been made profitable. The storage connection would be purged at six. The notice did not say what the room was meant to do with its people.

Lenna gave me a cloth tape because, she said, buildings were not the only things that needed room. I told her cloth stretched. She repaired the first inch with yellow thread and told me to remember it was missing. I remembered the missing inch more faithfully than I remembered to visit.

Something took the other end. Three tugs. The service signal we were taught before the Department replaced training with a video. Not a haunting. Not settling plaster. A person, behind a wall I had certified as empty. I stopped the report. Then I started it again. This time I said her name.
