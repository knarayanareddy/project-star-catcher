**Applies to:** episode scripts 101–106, Draft 1, 6 November 2026.  
**Purpose:** document the checked story logic, rather than merely declare that continuity is perfect. Automated checks cover structure and links; the narrative checks below are editorial judgments. Further drafts and performance may expose new problems.

## 4. Finance ledger

The credit is a fictional unit. These figures serve causal clarity, not a claim about real municipal economics.

### Four distinct streams

1. **Existing clinical fees:** cooperative obligations remain paid. Renewal care can continue during the contract dispute. The outside-provider session has a paid deposit from Mara; the reserve vote does not retrospectively make Nadi volunteer.
2. **Housing supplement:** municipal benefit administered through the group certificate. WellSpring improperly blocks it. Seventeen affected households need thirty credits per day each.
3. **Disputed administration fees:** held in neutral escrow under the contract. No character spends these funds on rent, care, a kettle, or wages during this season.
4. **Future local administration allocation:** message license 900/month + retention incentive allocation 600/month = 1,500/month. Nadi's additional paid transition-clinic plan is 1,200/month, including its small administrative role. The interim order permits that plan, subject to review. It is supplementary local service, not the entire neighborhood's healthcare budget.

### Housing bridge arithmetic

- 17 × 30 = **510/day**.
- Voted reserve cap: **5,100**, enough for ten days.
- Actual use: seven days × 510 = **3,570**.
- Unspent portion of allocation: **1,530**.
- By finale, five used days reimbursed: 5 × 510 = **2,550**.
- Allocated reserve balance restored/retained: 1,530 + 2,550 = **4,080**.
- Two used days still pending reconciliation: **1,020**.
- 4,080 + 1,020 = original **5,100** allocation once reconciliation completes.

The rest of the cooperative's reserves are not specified. Delayed window work establishes opportunity cost without claiming all building funds were exhausted. Mara's savings are a separate offer, not needed to make the ledger balance; she cannot buy forgiveness. No script promises her savings as the stable source of future care.

## 7. Objects, sound, and space

### Kettle

- Chipped appliance with loose handle and faulty stay-on switch. Isha holds it in 101; Martin uses it in 103.
- Handle tightened in 104.6; switch remains faulty. Do not animate a fixed switch in later scenes.
- Sponsor's replacement is refused and leaves with the rep.
- Packed in 104; appears unplugged in hearing room in 105 under appliance restriction. Nobody brews a distracting cup during testimony.
- Returned to apartment in 106; Mara holds switch while Isha gets cups. This exchange has been earned, not assigned as a symbolic duty by a speech.

### Recorder and authentic audio

At canteen shelf in 101, fragment played in 102, explicitly excluded from hearing in 104, carried home off-screen before 106. The same cough, pears line, and first request must match across 102 and 106. Keep the synthetic phrase out of the authentic message: similarity would accidentally suggest the fabricated file had truthful provenance. Playback ends once. No loop or ghost response.

### Other prop rules

Suri's tin holds originals, not snacks or magic evidence. Tessa's ledger uses distinct envelopes for care, escrow, and housing. Mara's tablet/lanyard cease to provide staff access after 104. Martin's work badge and phone recur. The third cup in 106 is blue, empty, and materially present; no apparition appears in its chair.

### Spatial plan

Claims booth and office floor can share a set; Pell's room needs audible clinic life beyond it. Canteen contains a door/screened clinical space with real privacy and a separate quiet kitchen. The hearing is a **municipal clinic annex**, not the canteen or WellSpring corporate office. Its glazed door directly faces the corridor used for the ninety-second recess. The Venn apartment is distinct from the canteen, established by a new location heading rather than a confusing redress with no explanation.

### Sound arc

Service chime: helpful transaction in 101; coercive policy notice in 102–103; conspicuously absent at 103 end; ordinary background in 105, no longer dictating a response. Finale retains city sound. “Quiet hour” disables building announcements, not other people's lives.

## 8. Antagonist, rights, and ending checks

- Pell protects a genuinely useful service. The scripts do not excuse his choices as inevitable; he repeatedly chooses financing through coercion instead of confronting management allocations.
- Vale can argue forcefully without a late false document or cartoon confession. The documents are real; disputed interpretation and responsibility require Pell's admission.
- Rao's order is local and interim. Existing care and voluntary refusal both survive. The order refers conduct for investigation; it does not convict Pell in a ten-minute contract hearing.
- Paid transition staff and a continuing supplement make the victory material. A roomful of hugs would not solve rent or payroll.
- National financing, long-term clinical outcomes, and final disciplinary findings remain unresolved. This is an ending with defined limits, not a cliffhanger withholding the immediate answer.
- Mara and Isha acknowledge their mother without bringing her back or converting her last words into a perfected farewell. Their new capacity is staying together through an imperfect evening.
