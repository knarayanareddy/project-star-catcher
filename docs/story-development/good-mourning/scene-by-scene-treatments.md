# GOOD MOURNING — scene-by-scene treatments

Draft 1. Scene IDs match the screenplay scene markers and continuity ledger. All time ranges are editorial targets. Each episode ends its story at 09:45 and reserves 09:45–10:00 for credits. The seven-day season clock runs from Monday to the following Monday; the finale advances two weeks.

## Episode 101 — Sorry for Your Convenience

**Dramatic question:** Will Mara close the awkward cases to protect her promotion, or investigate the comfort she has sold?  
**Beginning → end:** confidence in a helpful product → evidence that her own intimate relief was manufactured.

### 101.1 · 00:00–01:15 · Claims booth · Monday morning
Mara adjusts widower Martin Hale's condolence calls so he can sleep after night work. She treats the schedule, not his wish to be left alone, as the problem. Martin genuinely needs the Hold to keep working and does not want to lose his appointments. A fee breakdown visibly separates clinical care, administrative services, and a message add-on. Len's promotion board rewards completed renewals. Mara's skill feels humane until the scoring system frames that kindness as retention. Transition on the service chime into a kettle's failing click.

### 101.2 · 01:15–02:45 · Canteen kitchen · Monday evening
Isha uses Rena's chipped kettle, which boils only after someone holds the switch. Mara brings an annual household certificate to supper. Isha will not sign bundled terms linking housing support, Hold consent, and the mother's archive. Mara offers a release appointment as though it were an extension on paperwork. Isha points out Mara's twelve canceled attempts to release. Their conflict is not whether Rena died but whether facts without feeling count as coping. Mara leaves the signature blank but promises to return.

### 101.3 · 02:45–04:15 · Claims booth · Tuesday morning
Mara hears her mother's archived kettle phrase while reviewing her own comfort subscription. Another customer, Mrs. Dev, arrives with an apparently personal message containing exactly the same phrase. Distinct voices share the same script and template identifier. Mrs. Dev is pleased by her message; Mara cannot dismiss that benefit. Mara asks permission to use an anonymized source reference, not to expose Dev's bereavement. The product's claim of an authentic recording, not the existence of synthesis, is the issue.

### 101.4 · 04:15–05:45 · Staff kitchenette
Len explains standard “supportive reconstruction.” Mara asks whether reconstruction is disclosed. He points to an archival label that reassures rather than informs. A municipal dispute link and five-working-day hearing target appear in a routine complaint banner. Len is not a villainous confession machine: he wants a ticket closed because his own father's supplement depends on the office. Mara requests a source audit as the account holder and declines to certify a harmless duplicate.

### 101.5 · 05:45–07:15 · Mara's desk
With legitimate access to her own account and Dev's limited consent reference, Mara compares source dates and labels. Rena's message was generated nine months after Rena died and described as a recovered original. The source evidence can be exported with an audit hash. She saves her own record and the anonymized comparison; she does not download the office's private client database. An unread release reminder shows Mara's monthly clinic renewal is due today.

### 101.6 · 07:15–08:45 · Canteen kitchen
Mara brings the evidence instead of a signature request. Isha does not instantly welcome her: she has distrusted the voice for months. Mara admits she believed it. She requests permission to inspect their mother's household paperwork. Isha gives her a box but keeps an old handheld recorder aside. The audience sees a real recording exists without learning its full content. Their first agreement is small: no messages published in Rena's name without both sisters' consent.

### 101.7 · 08:45–09:45 · Claims booth, after hours
Mara removes Isha's household certification from her daily completion queue, losing a perfect score. She opens her own release history: twelve appointment slots, including rescheduled requests, canceled over eleven months. The cancellation authority is Pell. She files a municipal contract complaint, starting the process needed for episode 105, and books an independent assessment instead of her scheduled renewal. Ending: she now has two unfinished tasks she cannot turn into someone else's fault.

## Episode 102 — A Small Administrative Sadness

**Dramatic question:** Are these cancellations care, incompetence, or a profitable pattern?  
**Beginning → end:** suspected product fraud → documented financial obstruction, plus a modest lawful attempt to offer choice.

### 102.1 · 00:00–01:15 · Training room · Wednesday morning
Len's exercise requires staff to transform a bad sandwich into positive feedback. Mara requests archived staffing codes while appearing to participate. The scene reveals both her ease with corporate language and Len's vigilance about his father's treatment. Her access is plausible: she can inspect operational staffing codes but not all patients' charts. She arranges to see Pell before lunch.

### 102.2 · 01:15–02:45 · Pell's consulting room
Pell uses an actual patient's chosen renewal, with no identifying details, to defend the Hold. Mara agrees relief can help. His argument fails when her supposedly clinical cancellations refer to slot-reallocation codes. He approved a retention rule to keep funding stable and calls the next release “not yet.” Mara asks for a reason tied to her condition; he cannot supply one. No neural procedure is depicted as punishment or mind control.

### 102.3 · 02:45–04:15 · Canteen, paperwork table
Suri opens a biscuit tin containing the cooperative's original terms. Clinical refusal must not terminate rent support; administrative charges may be disputed separately; emergency municipal review can stay annual automatic renewal. Mara compares the contract with non-patient staffing data. Release capacity drops whenever retention targets are missed. Tessa, a resident accountant, is established checking the cooperative's reserve statement. The eventual money solution enters as ordinary governance, not late-arriving generosity.

### 102.4 · 04:15–05:45 · Canteen kitchen
While searching the permitted household box, Mara finds Isha's old renewal receipts. Isha had two treatments soon after Rena died, then stopped. Her supposedly simple anti-Hold stance fractures. She plays a few seconds of Rena's authentic recorder: irritation, a cough, a request to stop fussing. Mara cannot bear the sound and asks her to stop. Isha stops immediately. Consent begins inside their relationship before becoming a political demand.

### 102.5 · 05:45–07:15 · Clinic room
Independent clinician Nadi Sen assesses Mara's chosen deferral, explains gradual rebound, and books check-ins. Mara cannot supervise clinical care herself. Nadi agrees to an opt-in assessment session at the canteen, with paid staff and no group treatment spectacle. Mara pays a modest deposit; the cooperative can vote later on additional staffing. Renewal remains available. Mara resists the temptation to make her personal choice a universal program.

### 102.6 · 07:15–08:45 · Pell's doorway / canteen phone
Mara requests routine cooperative staffing from WellSpring. Pell declines, invoking continuity and approved premises. She provides Nadi's license and session plan; he still refuses. Because the session remains a registered care event under the group scheme, filing it generates an employer notification. This establishes how WellSpring learns about a lawful gathering without omniscient surveillance.

### 102.7 · 08:45–09:45 · Canteen noticeboard
The notice classifies outside-provider sessions as unpaid recovery time. Isha wants to cancel rather than cost residents wages. Mara circles the existing consent clause and includes a clear warning about the unresolved pay dispute on the invitation. They schedule Thursday afternoon across shift changes. No one is promised protection the organizers do not yet possess. Ending: the state of the problem has moved from two sisters' distrust to a neighborhood's material risk.

## Episode 103 — Terms and Conditions Apply

**Dramatic question:** Can the sisters offer a choice without controlling the people choosing?  
**Beginning → end:** a carefully voluntary session → economic retaliation and Mara's discovery that she designed part of it.

### 103.1 · 00:00–01:15 · Canteen entrance · Thursday afternoon
Isha's “Liberation Afternoon” sign alarms a resident. Mara replaces it with “Independent appointments.” Nadi explains separate options: renew, assess release, or defer a decision. There is no requirement to cry or disclose a loss. Len arrives as a declared compliance observer because the event was registered, not because someone secretly betrayed the group. Clinical conversations remain private.

### 103.2 · 01:15–02:30 · Canteen / screened consultation area
Mrs. Dev chooses continued treatment and asks for her old time slot. Another resident chooses release assessment without giving a speech. Len recognizes Dev as his father's neighbor and helps her check a transport time, briefly becoming a carer rather than a monitor. Isha stops an enthusiastic attendee from photographing consent forms. The politics becomes something she must practice, not announce.

### 103.3 · 02:30–04:30 · Quiet kitchen
Martin has consented to sit with Mara while waiting for Nadi; this is companionship, not unsupervised therapy. For two screen minutes neither speaks. Mara starts several habitual fixes—straightening a chair, reaching for a pamphlet, preparing a reassuring phrase—then abandons each. Martin rights the kettle switch, makes room, and does not cry on cue. The camera stays. The scene proves the season's central skill: remaining without filling the silence. No dialogue or sentimental montage may replace the duration casually.

### 103.4 · 04:30–06:00 · Canteen accounts table
A certificate freeze arrives, blocking the next housing supplement. Suri calculates the affected seventeen households. Nadi confirms patient care cannot be stopped merely because a group certificate is disputed, but she cannot invent money. Tessa pulls the reserve statement introduced in episode 102. Clinical risk and housing risk are kept distinct.

### 103.5 · 06:00–07:30 · Pell's room / canteen phone
Mara challenges the freeze. Pell calls it verification and offers expedited reapproval through uniform renewals. She asks him to put the link between clinical consent and subsidy restoration in writing. He will only send the policy notice, which is enough to add to the contract complaint. His paternalism becomes coercion in a concrete transaction.

### 103.6 · 07:30–08:45 · Kitchen, evidence preparation
Isha wants to publish appointment records to prove the injury. Mara prevents disclosure without residents' consent. They build a redacted evidence bundle using Mara's records, the contract, anonymized staffing data, and the freeze notice. Suri submits it through the complaint route already opened. Names are not casually blacked out on a live feed; each selected document is checked before upload.

### 103.7 · 08:45–09:45 · Canteen closing
Len shares the operational retention notice he is permitted to view. The label “unprofitable recovery cluster” has Mara's old design attribution. Mara admits authorship before Isha can discover it later. Isha asks who else her classifications hurt. Mara cannot answer yet. End on the blank list she will have to complete, not on an easy apology.

## Episode 104 — We Value Your Feedback

**Dramatic question:** Who pays for doing the right thing?  
**Beginning → end:** righteous publicity with no cash → a limited, voted bridge and staff willing to testify under material protection.

### 104.1 · 00:00–01:00 · Canteen counter · Sunday morning
An opportunistic sponsor offers equipment in return for exclusive, branded grief footage. Isha refuses. After he leaves she admits they needed the deposit. The scene prevents rebellion from becoming a clean identity detached from bills. The ugly kettle remains visibly in use rather than being replaced by glossy donated equipment.

### 104.2 · 01:00–02:30 · Accounts table
Tessa shows the actual ledger: seventeen households × thirty credits a day = 510 credits daily; a voted 5,100-credit reserve allocation buys ten days. Disputed administrative fees will stay in escrow; they cannot be spent twice. Clinical fees continue. The reserve is collective money and needs a resident vote. Suri gives up unilateral control of the proposal.

### 104.3 · 02:30–04:15 · Cooperative hall
Mara confesses that she designed the risk label, offers her records and savings, and does not ask to lead. Mrs. Dev declines to forgive her. Residents ask about repairs and staff pay. The reserve bridge and escrow motion passes narrowly, eleven to eight; Mara does not vote on her own accountability. A review date and spending cap are written on the motion. The group chooses a limited-term negotiator rather than appointing Suri forever.

### 104.4 · 04:15–05:45 · Pell's office
Pell offers Mara the promotion and a restored certificate if she signs a harmless-error closure. He says clinical staff could lose shifts if the contract unravels. His warning is credible, but he offers no comparable sacrifice from management. Mara reads the exact certification and refuses to deny harm. Her staff access is suspended; later evidence must therefore come from already exported personal records or Len's lawful access.

### 104.5 · 05:45–07:15 · Clinic corridor
Len hears that junior staff have proposed unpaid cover. Mara tells him the residents will pay for actual care. He brings operational invoices showing a message license and retention incentive allocation large enough to cover the proposed local transition hours if reallocated by contract order. These are not immediate cash; he insists on writing that distinction down. Len agrees to identify and explain his records at the hearing.

### 104.6 · 07:15–08:45 · Kitchen
Isha expects another explanation of Mara's good intentions. Instead Mara begins the list of households potentially affected by her classification and prepares a self-incriminating disclosure. Isha corrects a form, then silently feeds her. The two can cooperate without being reconciled. They agree the authentic recording will not be used as courtroom leverage or publicity.

### 104.7 · 08:45–09:45 · Canteen printer
The expedited hearing is confirmed for Monday 09:48, twelve minutes before the 10:00 annual lock. A notice warns that a filing alone does not pause renewal. The evidence bundle and original contracts are physically packed; Len confirms electronic copies are lodged. Suri sets the kettle by the meeting-room extension lead for the morning. Ending: a ticking administrative deadline whose remedy is already legally established.

## Episode 105 — Have You Tried Being Better?

**Dramatic question:** Can the group win consent without turning people who need relief into evidence against themselves?  
**Beginning → end:** a hearing framed as treatment versus freedom → a narrow order protecting both treatment and refusal.

### 105.1 · 00:00–01:15 · Clinic annex hearing room · Monday 09:48
Adjudicator Rao states the proceeding's limits and renewal deadline. Corporate counsel Vale presents Martin as a patient helped by the Hold. Isha refuses to make him the enemy. The hearing sits in a municipal clinic annex, establishing why Pell can reach the adjacent clinical corridor during a very short recess. The kettle is on a side table, not a functioning courtroom gag that delays judgment.

### 105.2 · 01:15–02:45 · Hearing room
Isha discloses her two renewals. She distinguishes a useful choice from indefinite coerced continuation. Mara supplies her own cancellation history and the label she designed. She consents to professional referral; residents' clinical files are not displayed. Their vulnerability is evidentiary and specific, not a contest in suffering.

### 105.3 · 02:45–04:15 · Hearing room
Suri supplies original terms; Tessa's signed arithmetic and Len's invoices separate housing reserve, care fees, escrow, and proposed future savings. Rao can stay the annual contract, protect the supplement during review, and order a local transitional service arrangement under the contract. Rao cannot abolish the Hold or adjudicate every patient's treatment. Vale accepts the logs exist but disputes what Pell's cancellation policy meant.

### 105.4 · 04:15–05:45 · Adjacent clinic corridor
A ninety-second recess. Pell sees the unpaid-shift list introduced earlier. Len removes his own volunteered shift: his father still needs someone to take him home. He refuses to subsidize management's moral awakening. Pell receives no new secret; he confronts the cost he already knows. He returns in time because the clinic is next door, not across the city.

### 105.5 · 05:45–07:15 · Hearing room
Pell authenticates his signature and states that retention targets, not individual clinical findings, canceled release slots. He admits that misleading archive labels were approved centrally, including by him. His testimony confirms interpretation and responsibility; the source exports were already real. Rao dictates the interim stay, orders removal of the subsidy-consent link, preserves patient choice, and refers managerial conduct for review.

### 105.6 · 07:15–08:30 · Hearing room, electronic filing
The clerk transmits the order; the group waits for enforceable acknowledgment. Len checks the existing portal, not a hacked back door. At 09:56 the contract status changes to STAYED, and housing payment resumes pending review. A later 10:00 lock indicator disappears. The group does not cheer over Martin's medical choice; each person checks the concrete thing most at risk.

### 105.7 · 08:30–09:45 · Clinic corridor
Nadi offers Mara an available assessment slot and the option of resuming treatment. Neither her employer nor the hearing requires refusal. Isha deliberately steps away. Mara sits with the question rather than giving a crowd-pleasing rejection. Ending: the first decision she cannot outsource to her profession, sister, or politics.

## Episode 106 — Please Stay on the Line

**Dramatic question:** What does the victory require on an ordinary day?  
**Beginning → end:** an order on paper → paid, imperfect care and a family able to share a room.

### 106.1 · 00:00–01:15 · Cooperative clinic desk · Two weeks later
Mrs. Dev wants her familiar appointment time, not a speech about the new service. Mara, now a paid administrator with restricted data access, cannot promise the old time but finds a workable one. Nadi sees residents privately. Renew, release assessment, and deferred decisions remain visible options. The payoff is a useful desk that does not pretend to own its clients.

### 106.2 · 01:15–02:45 · Accounts corner / waiting area
Tessa confirms the ten-day reserve bridge was used for seven days and partly reimbursed after the restored supplement cleared; the unused balance remains protected. Escrow is still sealed. Future reallocated administrative charges fund paid clinic hours under the interim order, not by voluntary staff sacrifice. Pell's suspension is announced without celebratory humiliation. Len brings his father to a chosen renewal and leaves on time to care for him.

### 106.3 · 02:45–04:15 · Consultation room
Mara confirms she chose deferral at the hearing and has continued supported release assessment. Nadi asks about sleep, functioning, and support, not ideological sincerity. Mara leaves future treatment open and books another appointment. Her return of grief has unfolded over days, not a judicial switch. Isha has not become her therapist or compulsory monitor.

### 106.4 · 04:15–05:30 · Clinic desk
Martin asks to cancel the messages while keeping his clinical appointment. Mara makes both changes independently and gives him a confirmation. He does not thank her for rescuing the neighborhood or absolve her old work. She starts to add a reassurance, then simply lets the transaction end. Earlier competence has changed its purpose.

### 106.5 · 05:30–07:00 · Venn apartment kitchen
The ugly kettle has returned from the canteen. Isha offers the real recorder; Mara agrees and can stop playback at any time. The sisters listen to Rena's last mundane, angry, affectionate message. Its early cough and irritation match episode 102's fragment. No secret instruction or legal evidence appears. The full message belongs to them, not the campaign.

### 106.6 · 07:00–08:30 · Same kitchen
Isha laughs at Rena's shopping instructions. Mara cries, then apologizes for making every meal an appointment. Isha does not grant global forgiveness. She asks Mara not to leave when dinner becomes quiet. Mara stays. A broken kettle switch requires an ordinary cooperative fix: one holds it while the other gets cups. The recorder is switched off rather than set to loop.

### 106.7 · 08:30–09:45 · Kitchen / cooperative corridor
Suri switches off the building's scheduled cheer announcement for the voted quiet hour. This is a limited, publicly scheduled action, not her new personal power. The city continues outside. The sisters set out three mismatched cups; the third is an acknowledgment, not an apparition. Mara does not tidy it away. End with two people drinking tea and an empty place they do not have to fill.
