In Lumen, we have excellent sleep figures. Ninety-eight percent settle before ten. The other two percent maintain the figures. For thirty-five years, I have been the voice that tells the town it is safe to close its eyes. Tonight I was meant to teach my replacement. His chair was empty. The bedtime was not.

Teo fixed the microphone that made my voice sound effortless. He had left his tools in order, which was how I knew he had not intended to leave. The Board said he was contributing additional rest. I had read that phrase often. It became less comfortable when I could see the outline his hands had worn into the desk.

There were thirty-one beds behind the nursery glass. Every sleeper had volunteered for a shift. None had volunteered for the extra days. Teo's chest rose. A thread of light connected his wrist to a moth above him. I reached for it, and the thread pulled tight. Even help can become another kind of tugging if you don't know what is holding on.

The moth opened its wings. There was Teo's workbench, small enough to cover with my thumb. Then a staircase to a room he had never built. They feed on dreaming. We know this. We print it on the school tours. We do not usually ask what happens when the dreaming cannot stop.

The old keeper's hood was not for ceremony. It was for darkness. One hour, every day, without interruption. Twenty-eight crossed-out hours lay under Harl's hand. I had thought we were borrowing a little light from the night. Apparently we had decided the night was not entitled to have any of its own.
