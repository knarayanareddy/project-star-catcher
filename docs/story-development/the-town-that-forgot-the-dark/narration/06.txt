In the morning, we listed people rather than percentages. Awake. Resting. Still being watched. Every line had a name beside it. Harl entered the canceled rest hours with his own mark attached. We had not earned the right to call this a success story before the people inside it had finished needing care.

A week later, all thirty-one had woken. Some were still too tired to go home. The keeper's decisions were under review. Two people now had to authorize an extension, and nobody could authorize canceling the colony's rest. These were local rules, not a new human nature. They would need people willing to notice when the ink stopped meaning anything.

We had less light to waste. Some work took longer. The bakery opened later and produced bread of no obvious ideological difference. People complained. I found this reassuring. They had begun complaining about actual inconveniences instead of being told that any inconvenience meant the town might die. We were learning the difference between a limit and a threat.

Teo did not become the new voice of bedtime. Not immediately. He wanted to repair the training system first. We separated the human invitation to rest from the moths' work signals. It was embarrassing how long we had called those the same thing. I gave him the handbook. I did not tell him which voice to use.

I left one page unread. Not a warning. Not the missing answer. A space at the end of the day when nobody had to be instructed how to feel about it. The town rested. The moths rested. I was still awake, listening to a place that no longer needed me to say it was peaceful before it could become quiet.
